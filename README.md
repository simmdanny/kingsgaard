# Kingsgaard

The medieval fantasy RPG: an explorable procedural world with villages and cities, caves, magic and dragons.
It runs on the same engine it grew from (towers, enemies, spells and heroes are still part of its world), as one page with an illustrated main menu.

## The character
Play opens the character creator (`src/menu/menu.js`), seven steps that can be taken in order with Back and Next or opened from the row of steps: Gender (Male or Female: the build of the body and the starting look; every item can be worn by either), Name (2 to 18 characters: letters first, then letters, spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and full stops; Next and Create Character refuse a bad one with a message), Head (five heads for him: classic, chiselled, broad, lean, soft; five for her: classic, heart, high cheekbones, long oval, soft round; six skin tones), Hair (his: short crop, messy, swept back, spiky, buzzed, shaved; hers: pixie cut, bob, long, bun, braid; both: tied back; eight colours), Clothing (Top 1, the base layer: shirt, short-sleeved shirt, undershirt, blouse, tunic, sweater; Top 2, worn over it: none, open jacket, vest, long coat, hooded top, open shirt, leather jerkin, cloak; bottoms: trousers, fitted breeches, loose trousers, knee shorts, skirt, long skirt; shoes: boots, tall boots, shoes, sandals, pointed shoes; a colour out of twelve for each), Accessory (exactly one: none, spectacles, wide hat, feathered cap, hood, circlet, earrings, pendant, scarf, eyepatch) and Confirm (a summary with a Change link per line, and Create Character). Every option is shown as a picture of the character wearing it (`pcSnap`: the stage of the preview draws into a render target, a few pictures per frame, about 9 ms each, cached); the large figure on the right changes with every choice, turns by itself or by dragging, and has a whole-body and a close-up view (chosen by the step). Randomize gives a look of the chosen gender out of things that go together (a style offered to that gender, layers of different colours) and a name when the field is empty; Reset gives the default look of the gender; Main menu leaves without saving anything. The arrow keys move through the items of a category; everything works by touch, in portrait windows (the figure above, the steps below) and in short landscape ones.
Gender decides what can be chosen. Every item of the catalogue says who can wear it (`g`: 'm', 'f' or 'mf'): the heads are made for one gender each, the hairstyles above are his, hers or both, the blouse and the two skirts are hers, everything else is worn by both. The rule is in the data and in `pcClean`, not only in the creator: the creator shows only the items of the chosen gender (and its arrows, its keys and Randomize go through those only), switching gender replaces what does not fit (keeping everything else), and a saved character with an item of the other gender (an old save, an edited one) is repaired field by field on loading with the default of its gender. The heads of the first catalogue (oval, round, square, long, heart, broad) become the nearest head of the saved gender.
The catalogue and the model are `src/pc.js`. The look is `PC` = {v:2, name, gender, head, skin, hair, hairC, top1, top1C, top2, top2C, bottoms, botC, shoes, shoeC, acc}: the items are ids of the lists in `PCX`, the colours indices into the palettes; `pcClean` turns anything into a valid look field by field (an unknown or missing item falls back to the default of the gender), and turns a character of the first creator (v1: a build, a hooded or bare head, two colours) into the nearest look of this one. Adding an item is a row in its list (and, for a new shape, a branch in `pcAssemble`); saves made before keep working because they store ids.
The character is built in seven files: `src/pc.js` (the catalogue, the saved look, materials, the preview stage), `src/pc2.js` (the landmarks of the body, the torso and its garment bands, the skeleton, skinning, spring bones), `src/pcbody.js` (the body sculpt), `src/pclp.js` (the low-poly heads), `src/pchair.js` (hair), `src/pcm.js` (the model: `pcAssemble`, called by `heroModel('pc')`) and `src/pcanim.js` (the animation controller). The body is a low-poly sculpt after the body reference (`pcbody.js`): rings of points at the landmarks of the body joined by flat planes that follow the anatomy - on the torso the chest and the line under the pectorals (her bust), the ribs, a narrow waist, the shoulder blades and the spine, the hips and a seat of two lobes (`PCT_M`/`PCT_F`: 16 rows of 9 mirrored columns, the quads split alternately so the planes meet in diamonds); on the arm the deltoid, biceps and triceps, the elbow and a forearm narrowing to a flat wrist (`PCBA_M`/`PCBA_F`); on the leg the thigh, the knee, the calf and the ankle (`PCBL_M`/`PCBL_F`); on the foot the heel, the ankle bones, the arch and the ball (`PCF_M`); hands with a palm, three-jointed fingers and a thumb, relaxed or closed round a grip (`pcHandGeo`). He is about seven heads tall (0.368 figure units to the crown, his head drawn at 0.91 of the unit), she about 6.7 (her head at 0.88, her figure 0.97 of his; drawn 3.45 times larger, `HS`): both are built in a shared layout and lifted by one vertical map that lengthens the trunk (`PCRY_M`/`PCRY_F`, `pcRemapY`; the weights are taken before it), and the sculpts are written in the finished heights and carried back into the layout (`pcRemapInv`). The clothes are laid on the sculpt itself: a torso garment is a band of the torso surface at an offset on the sculpt's own rows and columns (`pcShell2` -> `pcShellLP`), a sleeve or a trouser leg a tube on the limb's rows (`pcLimbTube`), a shoe the foot let out on a sole, a boot shaft a tube round the leg - so a shirt shows the planes of the body under it. The torso's top runs into the head's own neck surface (`pcBody2`, no seam); the necks stand under the skulls. It stands on a skeleton of `THREE.Bone` (root, spine, chest, neck, head; collarbones, upper arms, forearms, hands; thighs, shins, feet, toes) built in the bind pose: body, clothes, shoes and the head with its neck are skinned meshes whose weights come from where each vertex is and which part it belongs to (`pcSkinBuild`, `pcWTorso`/`pcWArm`/`pcWLeg`/`pcWFoot`; the neck of the torso and the neck of the head share one rule, so they stay together when the head turns); hands (relaxed and gripping), eyes, short hair, accessories and weapons are rigid parts on their bones. The heads are low-poly sculpts as well (`pclp.js`, `pcHeadLPM`): his after the head reference; hers the same sculpt made feminine (a shorter lower face, a narrower softer jaw, a finer chin, a rounder forehead, a flatter brow, larger eyes with lashes, thin arched brows, a smaller nose), their features simple shapes on their planes (`pcFaceLP`). Clothes are garments: hems, cuffs, collars and bands with rolled edges, creases inside the elbow and behind the knee, a top that blouses over the belt; skirts, the skirt of a tunic, the skirts of a coat and the cloak hang on chains of spring bones that the legs and the back push (`pcWChains`, colliders on the bones sized from the leg sculpt), keep clear of the legs at rest (`hipR`), and the cloth over a thigh takes a share of the thigh's weight (`withThighs`: from just under the belt the cloth over a thigh goes with it entirely - across the thigh's own width, fading beyond it - and below the knee with the shin too; the two thighs' shares are laid on together, so the cloth between the legs follows both alike; the back of the cloth mostly hangs free on its springs, since carried forward by a thigh driven up it would sink into the seat behind; in a tunic, as in a skirt or a long coat, the run and the sprint drive the knee a little less), so a striding leg carries a skirt instead of passing through it. A trouser leg follows its own leg wherever it reaches (`LEG(sd)`, `FOOT(sd)`: wide trousers cross the middle of the body, and the side of a point there must not choose the other leg). Every material is flat shaded (`PCLOW`; the fabric relief of the shader, `PCFAB`, is off in the low-poly look). About 7,000 to 10,000 triangles in some 39 draw calls. The body lab `tests/body-lab.js` draws the bare body (`cfg._bare`), its silhouette, posed frames of the controller and green-skin poke-through sheets.
Hair (`pchair.js`) is three layers: a structural cap of the head surface out to the hairline (thick where the style has volume, hanging straight from the widest part of the head for a bob or long hair), tapered locks laid over it along the flow of the style (down from the crown, back from the brow, round to a tie; they lift off at their tips and take the head's normal, so the hair shades as one mass with a broken outline), and the fine strands, which the hair material draws: a striation across the flow and two Kajiya-Kay highlights along it (`pcHairMat`: every hair vertex carries `flow` and `hst`). What hangs is skinned to chains on the head (the ends of a bob, long hair, a tail, a braid), each style with its own chains, stiffness, damping and limits; spring bones (`pcSpring`, `pcPhys`) lag, sway and settle and are pushed out of spheres on the head, neck, back and shoulders; only part of the figure's motion through the world reaches them (`inertia`), so running does not blow the hair flat. The controller (`pcCtl`) reads how the figure actually moves (speed, turning, acceleration of its parent) and what it does (the attack or spell, a knock-out, the weapon): a phase-driven gait in which each planted foot moves back exactly as fast as the body moves on (no sliding), landing on the heel and leaving from the toes, walk and run blended by speed, two-bone IK with the knee forward; the pelvis rises, sways, turns and drops, the chest turns against it, the arms swing against the legs, the head keeps looking ahead and leads into turns, the body leans with speed and acceleration and banks into curves; idle breathing, weight shift and looking about; the feet stay locked to the ground at rest and step back under the body when it turns on the spot or stops. Stances (empty hands, bow, sword) blend; the game's attacks and spells (`POSES`) are laid over the upper body while the legs walk on, the sword has its own cuts (`PCA_SW`). The character's weapon is the bow (a ranger) or the sword (`HCL.blade`, fights hand to hand): `meta.pcw`, X changes it, R draws or puts it away (the bow goes onto the back, the sword into its scabbard; an attack draws it, eight quiet seconds put it away), C walks instead of running in the open world. The creator's preview is animated (breathing, the hair answers when it is turned). Tests: `tests/head-lab.js` (heads and bodies on a neutral ground, views from the eyes to the whole figure), `tests/k-dbg.js` (tinted pictures, spring and animation runs on the preview stage), `tests/k-game.js` (the character driven with the keys in the open world).
The rules of the game see an archer with the numbers of the first free hero (`HB.pc`, a copy of Robin's, not in `HEROES`, so no archive, shop or tower list ever shows it) with progress of its own (`hs('pc')`, level 1 at the start of every new character). The premade heroes stay in the game for everything that used them (the towers, the scrolls, the archive code); the Heroes tab of the hub is hidden.
Saves: the run (`kingsgaard.run`) holds `pc` (the character) and `pcp` (its level, xp, perks, slots) next to the seed and the state of the world; the older adventures that were set aside are in `kingsgaard.slots`. Play never touches a save: the realm is forged first and only when that has worked is the adventure in progress set aside (never deleted: it is in Load) and the new one begun, which `newGame` writes at once; Back, Escape, a wrong name and a realm that cannot be forged leave every save as it was. Load lists the latest adventure and the ones set aside with the name of the character, its level, the realm, villages found, gold, time played and when; a damaged or old save is shown as such and does nothing; choosing one sets the adventure in progress aside and puts the chosen one in its place, so nothing is overwritten. Loading builds the world from the seed of the save (the same world, not a new random one) and puts the state of the save back (`owLoad`). A save from before the creator has a premade hero: it stays the hero of that save (`pcRestore`), nothing is converted and nothing is lost. A save of the first creator (v1 look) loads with its character converted to the nearest look of the new one (name, gender, level and progress kept). What is not the character's (crowns, gems, the collection of scrolls, scraps) is shared by all saves, as it always was. The scale of the walking hero is `HS` = 3.45 and of the villagers 2.78 (`owMakeVillager`, with a hash-based variation of -4 to +4 %): both +10 % over the last version; nothing else is scaled and the collision radius of the hero (0.27 m) is the same. `tests/menu-flow.js` drives the real buttons through the whole flow (37 checks: every step, both layers on the preview and on the walking hero, every field of the look through save and load, the v1 conversion, damaged looks).

## Main menu
Play (confirms before replacing an adventure in progress, and sets the old one aside under Load), Load (latest adventure and set-aside ones, import of a `KK1:` code), Settings (the game's own settings window; its close button reads "Back to menu").
The 3D world is not built at start-up: Play forges it with the existing generator, then shows the existing hero hub; Load builds it from the save.

## Interiors
Ordinary houses, row houses, the tavern, the merchants, the smith, the carpenter, the storage houses, the larger houses and the passages of Tier 5 can be entered, and the great hall is furnished and seen through its windows; the way in is generated from the same numbers as the walls and the terrain, and the interior is generated from the building's own architecture. See `src/interior/README.md`.

## The world: infinite, streamed round the player (src/world/, v42; the hilly grasslands and 4 km regions v46)
The open world has no edge. It is a pure function of the seed and the place (`src/world/wgen.js`): any piece of it is generated the same whichever way it is reached, and only what is near the player exists - the ground, the water, the landmarks, the villages, the roads, the plants, the occupancy grid. Walk on and the world goes on.

**Layers.** The world is built in layers (v46): the **biome** - for now the grasslands, the only one - is split into **regions** of at least about 3 x 3 km, and the regions will get **foliage styles** (forests, fields ...) later.
- **The grasslands** stand high and roll in vast hills: the floors of the valleys lie 30-95 m above the water (`wgBase`, a slow field), and on them the hills (`wgHills`: three octaves of noise from 2.8 km down, the big one leading so the slopes stay long - a hill a kilometre or more across rises 100-300 m, mostly under 20 degrees, every slope walkable; the lowest quarter cut off into broad valley floors where streams wander and villages and roads find room; low swells over everything so no floor is dead level). How big the hills are changes slowly from place to place (downs to great hills). Only the elderglade lies low.
- **Regions**: a soft Voronoi of 4 km cells (each site within a quarter cell of its lattice point, so a region is at least about 3 km across; neighbours of one kind make one bigger region - but never two elderglades: only every other cell each way may be one, so each elderglade is one region round one site, a fifth of the land), warped borders about 500 m wide (`wgRegions`). The start is the middle of a highlands cell (the keep stands in a valley among the hills; the mountain ranges keep about a kilometre back from it), and the four cells beside it hold the floating plateau, the elderglade and the highlands again, so every kind is within about 2 km.
  - **Highlands** (picture 13; the old "spires"): horn peaks - anisotropic cones whose intersections are knife-edge ridges and cols, in ranges (a massif mask), the faces broken by ribs and gullies, a few needles far taller and steeper, fluted - standing on the hills; snow on the upper slopes, off the steepest faces, in streaks down them.
  - **Floating plateau** (pictures 14, 15, 16; the old "tablelands"): plateaus in tiers (about 80 m each) on lower hills (half height), every tier a grassy top ending in a sheer cliff (its width in metres is the same everywhere: the slope of the tier field is measured), a talus apron at the foot of every cliff, wide gorges with stepped walls, pillars standing by the cliffs, sinkholes with pools in the tops, natural arches; the clouds of the pictures are left out.
  - **Elderglade** (picture 12; the old "ring lakes"): low land at the world's water level, flooded into wide lakes, islands and long turquoise shallows, rocky hills (clusters of rounded knolls, `wgKnoll`: a lobed outline, a smooth dome steepest at another height on each side, so the rock shows in curved patches, never a straight cut or a band) and the great stone rings (100-300 m across) standing in the water and on the shores; an elderglade is larger than the other regions (its distances count `WG_CFG.gladeK` (0.74) of theirs and its site keeps near its cell's middle: 4.2-5.2 km across, as a square of its area); the land of the other regions falls to it across the border, the streams in falls. Each elderglade has one **Great Elder Circle** (below).
- The meadows of the first version (the "plains" region) are no region now: their place in the region weights stays (always 0). The forest overlay still darkens the ground (it will hold the trees).
- `WG_VER` (9 since v59: the rock look's Great Elder peaks; 8 since v58: the river look's banks; 6 since v56: the Great Elder Circle's mountain of two peaks) is the version of the generator; a save keeps it (`ow.gv`), and a save made by another version keeps its character, level and things but its villages start afresh (they were other villages).

**Water.** A jittered 96 m lattice of the land's heights: every point drains to its lowest neighbour; how many points drain through a point (counted upstream, `wgAcc`) makes a stream (1-3 m) or a river (up to 15 m); a pit becomes a lake. The water level along a stream is a running minimum of the ground, so a stream that reaches a cliff falls over it - a waterfall. Streams carve their channels (with a shallow wet margin of `WG_MG` (2.2 m) either side, wider than the ground's lattice, so a bank never rises through the water) and shape their banks (raised where the land is lower than the water, so water never spills where it is drawn), lakes their basins. Rivers always run downhill: the water level along every stream never rises, one stream ends at the level the next begins, and where the land along a stream stands higher than its water (a hill in its path) the stream cuts a valley through it - the land beside it may stand no higher than its water, the bank, and half a metre per metre beyond (`WG_VS`, a soft minimum; `s.cut`, `s.vw` from how deep the cut is, up to 150 m either side), never a slot with walls (v48). A lake is dropped where it would reach the world's water or a lower lake, so two waters at different levels never meet.
**Drawing the water (v48).** Lakes and the elderglade's water are drawn per chunk where their level stands over the ground (`wtBuild`); a cell whose corners' levels differ is drawn only when it is one stream falling along its flow, so no sheet is ever stretched between two waters. Streams are drawn by `src/world/wriver.js`: a ribbon along each stream's own curve at its water level (three vertices across, deepest in the middle and fading to nothing at both edges over the wet margin), built per 256 m cell within reach of the chunks that carve the channels, ending where a lake or the world's water takes over. The water material (`wtWaterMat`, stylized): clean colour by depth - turquoise shallows, blue-green, deep blue - nearly opaque from a couple of metres and fading out over the last few decimetres at the shore, with a thin clean line of foam that breathes slowly; broad slow ripples that fade with distance (lakes almost still, rivers streaked along their flow), a soft sky tint at low angles, a small sun glint; steep water (rapids, falls) white. No fine noise to shimmer. Reeds grow along the streams.

**Ground shading** (`wtMaterial`, the game's Phong lighting - sun, shadows, lamps, fog - with the colour worked out per pixel from four reads of a tileable noise texture, every channel a different scale): grass in broad drifts between yellow-green and blue-green, patches of light and dark, deeper in hollows and lighter on crests (a cavity per vertex); rock where it is steep (and always near a cliff: a rock floor from the steepest neighbour), with striations and streaks down the faces, broad stains, cracks, strata, moss on its ledges, its relief from a height pattern (bump mapping); the colour of the rock is the region's (cool grey spires, warm beige tablelands, dark mossy lake rock). Detail finer than a pixel is left out (no shimmer).

**The terrain.** Level-0 tiles: 128 m of a 2 m lattice (`wtTile`); `owH` reads the very triangles they are drawn with. Chunks for the eye: a quadtree round the camera, 128 m x 2^L, 64 x 64 cells, skirts (`wtUpdate`), out to about 6 km. The slow fields of the 16 m lattice (region weights, base, forest, the hills' size and warp) are kept in blocks of 32 x 32 points once worked out (`WG_LB`, 700 blocks a thread, least recently used let go; v50): tiles, chunks, the hydrology, streams, sites and roads all ask for overlapping rectangles, so a terrain tile is made about a third faster and a fresh village site about twice as fast - the same numbers (`world-sig2` identical). Grids are generated by three web workers (`src/world/wworker.js`: the same files as the main thread, so the same numbers; the settlements' stamps are sent with each request as plain data, `wgStampApply`); the tile under the player is never waited for. Far sight (`src/world/wdepth.js`): every vertex farther than 900 m from the camera is drawn nearer along its own line of sight (the order along a ray is kept, the picture is the same), so mountains 6 km away fit a far plane of 2.1 km with the near plane at 0.1 m; the open world's fog is gradual (v53): over the true distance the air is clear out to 58 % of its end (`WD.S`), then thickens - slight at first, stronger and stronger - until nothing shows at the end (a smoothstep, so there is no wall of fog and no edge where the land stops). The end follows the Render distance setting (`owFogEnd`: `OW_CFG.fogK` (7) times it, farther with the free camera's height): 80 to 2500, so clear to 690 m and fog to 1.2 km at the default 170, clear to 10.2 km and fog to 17.5 km at the far end; the setting shows both numbers. Past the end no terrain is drawn, and the cameras leave the fog alone. Round a Great Elder Circle (v56) the haze has a top (`WD.SEE0`, `WD.SEE1`: only the top of the ring shows over it from afar), takes the sky's colour in the direction looked (`uZen` = `WD.zen`, set by `owSkyDome` every frame) and fogs the circle as one object (`uSee`, `uGreat`, `WD_PED`) - see The Great Elder Circle. The things of the world (villages, their labels and lamps, villagers) keep their own reach (`OW_CFG.viewDist`, the render distance up to 400). The free camera roams the whole world (the old board's bounds kept it within about a kilometre of the start).

**The hydrology from the workers (v54).** The first time a place is touched its hydrology - the heights of the 96 m lattice, where each point drains, how much drains through it (`wgAcc` counts 40 steps upstream, kilometres off), its lakes and its streams - was worked out on the main thread whenever a far village's site, a landmark, a road or a tile's trees asked for it: 50-160 ms a block of 8 x 8 points, and a block pulls in every block upstream of it, so one far landmark could cost half a second or more. The workers now work it out ahead (`WHP` in `wterrain.js`): the blocks round the player out to as far as the game will ask (`WHP.R`, set in `wstFrame`: the sites two cells past the roads' 1.5 km, the landmarks out to `WF.far`, with a long Detail distance the sites out to its rings of cells), nearest first and towards where the player is heading, one block a job. Each worker keeps to its own squares of 3 km (a block's neighbours and what drains into it are then mostly in its own cache), at most two are at it at once (the main thread keeps a core), and the chunks of ground come first (a planned block only goes to a worker with at most one chunk in hand). A worker packs a block in full (`wgHydPack`: per point its height, where it drains, how much drains through it, its pit's lake and its stream's curve) and the main thread takes it in whole (`wgHydPut`): the same functions, so the same numbers, bit for bit - the world does not change (`WG.ep`, one more every `wgInit`, drops what comes back for an older world). What can wait - the far villages' sites (`owDetailRoads`), the landmarks past 1.5 km and the Great Elder Circles (`wfUpdate`), the trees past 600 m (`trwView`) - asks `wtHydReady` first and waits for its blocks, which are asked for first; only a lost worker (30 s) lets the main thread work one out (a block the main thread works out pulls in everything upstream of it). A forced view (`owView(true)`) makes the landmarks within 2.6 km at once; the rest come over the next frames. Also fixed: the trees' time budget (`trwView`) counted only the steps that built something, so when roads were added and every tile was looked at again (about two thousand at 3 km) all of them went in one frame - 60-170 ms every eighth frame while the land filled; every tile looked at counts now.
- Checks: `tests/hyd-check.js` (in a page with no world: for 6 seeds a worker's blocks == the main thread's, bit for bit; the land, streams, lakes, village sites, landmarks and level-0 grids read with the workers' blocks - all of them, and every other one - == worked out on the main thread alone; `__hc.grids` compares the game's own workers' grids with the main thread's after a fill) and `tests/hyd-fill.js` (the main thread's frame times while the land fills after the setting is raised: the world built and finished at the defaults, then the Render and Detail distance raised).
- Measured (seed 1 at the keep, this machine, `__hf.run({settle:{rd,dd}})`, medians/p95/p99/worst of the main thread's work per frame during the fill): 3 km (Render distance 430, Detail distance 3000) v53 median 23.8 ms, p95 163, p99 342, worst 867, 126 frames over 100 ms -> v54 (four runs) median 18.8-22.2, p95 41-56, p99 73-108, worst 165-238, 5-23 frames over 100 ms; 10 km (1430, 10000) v53 median 35.9, p95 156, p99 311, worst 1425, 168 over 100 ms, filled in 83 s -> v54 (two runs) median 25.8-28.1, p95 72-82, p99 116-133, worst 195-329, 26-45 over 100 ms, filled in 61-74 s. The main thread now works out about 20-25 blocks of hydrology itself in the 3 km fill (300 or more before), 72-77 in the 10 km one. What is left in the slow frames: level-0 tiles made on the main thread, a village's block builds, the ring layouts' ground samples (`wgHeightRaw` builds a context for every point). Travelling 4 km at the default settings changes little (the land within about 3.5 km of the keep has its hydrology from the start; the worst frames there are village builds): p99 50 -> 46 ms, the world's own spikes on the way 5 -> 2.

**Settlements and roads** (`src/world/wsite.js`): a cell of 720 m holds at most one village; its chance and size depend on the region (the highlands have the most and the biggest, in the valleys among their hills); the cells within about a kilometre of the keep always have one, and a big one is likely; the site is the flattest dry spot of a few dozen tries inside the cell, away from streams, lakes and landmarks (the flattest candidate that is dry wins), and a size that finds no ground tries the next size down (the land rolls: a village levels a few metres of it). The keep stands on the flattest dry spot within 260 m of the middle. Each village is joined to its nearest neighbours (and the villages near the keep to the keep) by A* over a cost grid of the land (slopes cost, cliffs and water are closed, streams can be crossed at a price), smoothed; the ground under a road is smoothed along it. Where a road crosses a stream it keeps above the water and a plain level deck carries it (the bridges are for later). Villages get names from a pool of first and second halves (Blackthorpe, Elmfield ...).

**Streaming, game side** (`src/world/wstream.js`): the occupancy grid is a window of 2048 cells round the player, moved by whole tiles when the player has gone 192 m from its middle; a tile fills its cells when it is made (steep ground and deep water closed, then the roads, the landmarks' feet and the crossings). A village is built when the player comes within 560 m (its tiles and roads first) and unloaded beyond 780 m: what changed in it is kept by its key while it is away and saved with the run (`ow.gv` = `WG_VER`, `ow.vs`); its streets, squares and the halves of its roads leave the ground with it. The merged meshes of its buildings are made one 30 m cell a frame. While a village is built its kit geometry is merged lazily (`wstLazyParts`: a geometry is merged the first time anything reads it, or a few milliseconds a frame from a queue), so a city's build went from about 0.8 s to about 0.55 s. Boulders lie on the tiles near the player (more on the talus, the shores, in the spires and the tablelands; the cliff rock, moss on top). A save of the old, fixed world keeps its character, level and things; its villages start afresh (they were other villages).

## Chiselled stone (src/world/wrock.js)
(v57: the cliff dressing below - walls of separate blocks - is replaced by the cliff sheets and the boulders of The world's look; the ground's chiselled material, the rings' stone and `wrkShape` remain, and wrock.js schedules the sheets.)
The stone of the world is the chiselled stone of the meadow study (a cartoon cliff of the user's liking): clean flat facets, no texture that repeats.
- **The ground material** (`wtMaterial`, wterrain.js) draws all rock this way. Each facet (a triangle of the ground, of a block, a boulder, a ring) is lit as one plane, its normal taken from the screen derivatives of the world position, and has one value from its own normal: lighter facing up, a step from facet to facet. Strata run in quiet bands a few metres high, each with its own tint, higher in the tablelands and spires. The stone is the region's own: cool grey spires, warm tablelands, dark mossy lake rock, warm grey elsewhere. The meadow's grass covers facets that face the sky. Far off the facets soften a little. The striations, streaks, cracks and rock bump-mapping are gone; snow keeps its streaks and relief, and the two face-texture reads now happen only where there can be snow. Its cost was measured against plain Phong at 1024 x 768 in the world lab (fastest of seven runs per material): within about 0.5 ms on cliff, spire, plains and far views, the same as the old rock.
- **Cliff dressing near the player.** A 2 m lattice cannot be rugged, so cliff faces (steeper than about 53 degrees) within `WRK.R` (200 m) get chiselled blocks, a merged mesh per level-0 tile drawn with the ground material (aA = region, aB.w = 1). Blocks are lined up along the contours where the face crosses terrace levels. Each block's top sits at its level, as a strip of meadow; its face reaches down the whole step, to the level below or the foot of the cliff. Steps follow the cliff's height (3.2 m, 6.4 m or 9.6 m, with blocks to match), so a tall tableland wall is a few big bands. Some crags and tall slabs break the rows, broken pieces lie on ledges, and blocks thinner than a step are left out.
- **What gets dressed.** The elderglade gets no blocks (v48): its rock is sculpted - rounded knolls and outcrops, shaded smoothly (no per-facet speckle or strata, a soft edge against the grass; `gRk` in the ground material) - where stacked blocks read as raw cuts. The spires get no blocks: their horn peaks are steep cones under snow, where blocks read as rubble, so their stone is the ground's own chiselled rock. Within `WRK.near` (90 m) blocks cast shadows; farther tiles keep only the tall cliffs' blocks - past `WRK.full`, which the Detail distance pushes out to 600 m (every block of every cliff out to it); at most 340 / 110 blocks a tile.
- **Streaming.** A tile is searched a few milliseconds a frame (`wrkGen` is a generator, the time checked after every lattice cell) and its mesh is made in a frame of its own. Measured in the game at the tablelands of seed 3: 1.2 ms a frame on average, 12.9 ms at worst while a big tile's mesh is made; about 80k rock triangles in view. Tiles are dropped beyond reach or when their level-0 tile goes.
- **Light on each stone.** Each block has light falling off as it would (`wrkOcc`, baked per vertex): its lower part, where it meets the ground, its sides set back between its neighbours (the wall's crevices) and what faces down are darker. It goes in as aB.y, which the material reads as its cavity, up to about 60 per cent darker in a deep crevice. Each block also has its own tint and brightness (aB.z, cooler or warmer; aB.w = 1 marks a block, where the ground's rock floor never goes past 0.9), so a wall reads as many stones, not one pattern. Boulders get their template's occlusion (low down, underneath) as their cavity.
- **Rings and arches** (wfeat.js; the rings are drawn by wring.js, below) are cut in big uneven facets (72 x 9 near, 40 x 6 far, their sections jittered) and drawn as the same stone.
- **Boulders** (wstream.js) keep their places, sizes and draws; only their six templates are chiselled stones (`wrkBoulderTpl`).
- **Visual only.** Every block draws from its own stream, seeded by where it stands; heights, sites, grid marks, props and saves are untouched. `tests/world-sig2.js` matches `tests/world-sig2-base.json`, and with `build:true` seed 3's villages (325 buildings) are identical to the build before.
- **Hooks.** `wfInit`, `wfUpdate` and `wfReset` (wfeat.js) call `wrkInit`, `wrkView` and `wrkReset`, so the game and the world lab both get the dressing.

## The stone rings of the elderglade (src/world/wring.js)
Each ring is its own. `wfSite` (wfeat.js) still decides where a ring stands, how big it is and which way it faces, and the cells it closes round its feet (wsite.js, wstream.js) are the same; `wfMesh` hands the ring to `wrMesh`, which draws it.
- **Whole, broken and ruined** (v48): about a third stand whole, two in five are broken, a quarter are ruined - only two stumps stand, a fifth to four fifths of the ring's height, among three to five fallen pieces and big broken blocks (`tests/eg-check.js`: 31-33 / 41-47 / 22-27 % over six worlds, all three near the start in every one).
- **Broken rings**: the top has broken off. What stands is the lower part, about a half ring, and its two horns end at different heights (one higher, one lower), each in a rough slanted break - the edge pulled out unevenly along the ring and closed by a rough cap, the horns a little thinner towards it. One to three of the fallen pieces lie half sunk beside the ring (or one across its foot), with rubble (`wrkShape` stones) round its feet and the pieces.
- **Taken back by the land**, from a little moss to wholly overgrown (`ov`): moss on the stone, which takes what faces up first and creeps down the sides in patches, more near the ground and the water (aA.w; the ground material reads it on a block, so the cliffs' blocks, with aA.w = 0, are unchanged); earth heaped where the stone goes into the ground and along the fallen pieces, with the land's own grass and colours on it; bushes (some in flower) and small trees on the top and on the breaks; bushes, tall grass and now and then a small tree round the feet; reeds where a foot stands in shallow water; vines hanging from the underside.
- **Size and shape:** thickness from three quarters to one and a quarter of `wfSite`'s; about one whole ring in four sunk deep, so only an arch shows; about a third lean; some have a small ring (20-50 m across) beside them, leaning away or nearly fallen, half buried, sometimes broken too.
- **Light:** the stone is marked as a block (aB.w = 1), so it takes the cliffs' light: darker underneath, on the inside, near the ground and in the hollows (aB.y), with a tint that wanders along the ring (aB.z).
- **Cost:** a ring is laid out once, a little before it comes into view (`wrView`, from `wfUpdate`: about 100-250 ground samples from `wgHeightRaw`, a few milliseconds a frame), so making its mesh builds only its far level (about 1-4 ms); its near level (vines, grass, the finer plants and stone, within 7 R) is built a few milliseconds a frame when the camera comes near. The plants are one more mesh (the foliage's flat vertex-colour material). The far level keeps the stone, the earth, the big rubble, the bushes and the trees (no vines or grass).
- **Visual only.** Every choice comes from the ring's own hashes (salts 330-351 on its cell, and streams seeded by them); heights, sites, props and saves are untouched (`tests/world-sig2.js` matches). The fallen pieces and the plants have no collision, like the boulders.
- **Feet** (v47): the grid closes a ring's cells where its stone really meets the ground (`wrFeet`: the contacts of the layout's frame, `wrFrame`; a tube going in slantwise leaves a long oval) - before, they were closed at a whole radius out along its line while its real feet stood at about 0.6-0.77 of it, so the hero walked through them. A tile asks for the landmarks that can reach it before it fills its grid, so the feet are closed however the player got there.

## The Great Elder Circle (v47, `WG_VER` 4; its mountain of two peaks v56, `WG_VER` 6: src/world/wgen.js `wgGreat`, `wgMtn`, wfeat.js `wfGreat`, wring.js)

- **Where:** the middle of the region is found (the mean of its core within 2.8 km of its site); the mountain stands 380-700 m off it - never on it - in a direction of the region's own (the first of ten, round by the golden angle, that keeps the mountain's body in the core and most of its foot in the region; smaller if none would). ## Detail distance: the land seen from afar as it is close up
Settings, Detail distance (`meta.dd`, 200 to 10000 m in steps of 50; 200, the default, is the game as it always was): how far the land is drawn in its full detail, so a far meadow or cliff looks as rich as a near one and the land can be enjoyed from afar - like distant horizons. The render distance (how far the air reaches) is a setting of its own; nothing is drawn past the end of the air, where it would only be fogged out (the distance used is at most `owFogEnd`: about 1.2 km at the default render distance, 17.5 km at its far end). The setting's text says where the fog stops it (`owDdText`: "3000 m (Horizon), the fog stops it at 1.1 km") and to raise the Render distance too.
- **What it sets** (`owDetailApply`, every frame from `owView`; the world's own values come back when it is turned down): the grass carpet past the near grass (`GRS.F.R`, below); the chiselled stones of the cliffs out to it (`WRK.R`), every block of every cliff out to `WRK.full` and their far levels (`WRK.far`, below); the boulders out to it but never nearer than their own 420 m nor further than 1.2 km (`WROCK.R`); the shape of the ground in finer chunks further out (`WT.K`: 1.4 at 200 m, 2.2 at 1 km, 2.6 at 3 km); the bigger plants - shrubs, saplings, ferns, reeds - out to 2.5 times their own distance (the Foliage setting's), thinning to a quarter at the far end, the small ones staying near (`OW.folK`, `folFar`).
- **The grass carpet far off** (v55, `src/grass.js`, `GRS.F`): the near grass (16 m cells, 36 m at high foliage) goes on as a far carpet out to `grsFarR(D)` - 0.3 m per metre of the setting past 200 m, at most 320 m (the Foliage setting scales it with the near grass: 178 / 240 / 320 / 409 m), none at Standard. Cells of 64 m, one instanced mesh each of a plain tuft (four broad blades, a triangle each; the clover patches are its lighter, yellower plants), placed by the same rules as the near grass (`grsSite`: never on a path, a square, a building, a field, water, a stream or steep ground). The density wanted falls off as the distance (`grsRho`: 1.4 candidates a square metre at the near grass's edge, half of it at twice that distance): seen at a slant, a metre of far ground is narrow on the screen while a plant on it stands as tall, so a carpet thinning as the distance covers the ground as much far off as near; the farther plants are up to half again as big, and the carpet fades out over the last fifth. Each plant carries its own density (the i-th candidate drawn in a cell stands for a density of (i+1) per cell area, in its instance colour's third channel); the shader keeps it where the density wanted at its distance is above that, so it thins smoothly as the camera moves, with no steps between cells (three.js gives a Lambert material no `cameraPosition`, so the camera is its own uniform, `uGC`). A cell draws only the plants its nearest corner could want (`InstancedMesh.count`). A cell is grown to the density of a distance 30 % nearer than where it is and grown on, from where its own seeded stream stopped, when the camera comes near enough to want more (`grsFarGrow`: the plants it has stay, only the new ones are worked out), a fraction of a millisecond a step (reading the ground in a village is slow, about 40 us a point) within 1.5 ms a frame (0.4 while the near grass bakes). A village built nearby takes its cells away (`wstInvalidate` -> `grsFarInvalidate`), so they grow again round its streets and houses. Cosmetic only, like the near grass.
- **Every stone of a far cliff** (v55): out to `WRK.full` (`OW_DD.full`: the setting, at most 600 m; `WRK.near`, 90 m, at Standard) a tile keeps every block of every cliff - the low ones (steps of 3.2 m) and the broken pieces on the ledges too, at most 340 a tile - where before only the tall cliffs kept theirs past 90 m (at most 110). Shadows stay within `WRK.near`.
- **The far levels of the stones** (src/world/wrock.js, `wrkLod`): a tile's level is 0 within `WRK.near` (shadows), else 1 + 2 x its facet level + 1 where only the tall cliffs keep their blocks (past `WRK.full`). The facet levels (`WRK.far`: [] unless this setting sets it, so the default is unchanged): out to 250 m the 80-facet stones they always were; past 250 m (when the setting is past 300 m) cut from the 20-facet ball, past 1 km from a 12-facet box - a few pixels across there, they look the same; past `far[2]` half of them, evenly (a hash of the place, the place staying taken; no edge sets it now). So the default's far tiles are level 2 (the old level 1, the same stones: checked byte for byte against v53). None casts a shadow past `WRK.near` (as before). A tile keeps its level for 40 m past an edge, so walking along one does not rebuild it back and forth.
- **Faster stones** (v55, the same stones byte for byte): a stone works each corner of its template out once from a map made once per template (`wrkIcoU`; it used to find shared corners through a Map of rounded positions, for every vertex of every stone), and the blocks already kept are looked up by number keys (they were strings, thousands a tile) - a tile is made about 40 % faster; with the 20-facet stones past 250 m a far full tile takes about a third of the time. Their vertices stay only on the graphics card: a far mesh is drawn once wherever it is (not culled until then, `wrkSeen`) and its arrays are let go once sent (`wrkFree`).
- **Out to 10 km** (far_patch, 2026-10-13):
  - Since v53 the air is the Render distance's alone (it no longer stretches to the Detail distance): the Detail distance draws out to the end of the air at most. The terrain's top chunks reach the end of the air (`wtWant`: 3 x 3 to about 4 km, 11 x 11 at 17.5 km), and the landmarks reach 16 km (`WF.far`). Measured at the far end (17.5 km), seed 1 at 650 m over an elderglade: the render 4.8 ms a frame, 1.27 million triangles.
  - The level-0 tiles, and everything that needs them (the stones above, the tile cap, the villages' roads), stop at 3 km (`OW_DD.tiles`). Past it, out to the setting, the tall cliffs' blocks are made from the ground's own coarse chunks (`wrkFar` / `wrkFarGen` in wrock.js): one block for each steep cell of a tall face (two on a taller one), the size of the cell (16-64 m), so the far cliffs read as stepped stone.
  - No thinning: `OW_DDF` has no 2 km edge any more, so every stone is kept out to 3 km. `WT.K` stops at 2.6.
  - Far stones and rings are drawn past the camera's far plane. Three.js culled everything farther than 2.1 km (`camera.far`), so whatever the setting, the far stones and the ordinary rings never showed past it. Now `wrkCull` and `wrCull` cull by the terrain's frustum, which has no far plane (`_wtFr`).
- **The land they stand on:** the level-0 tiles within the distance are asked for, nearest first (`owDetailTiles` lists the missing ones every 10 frames; once none is missing it looks again only when the hero has gone 32 m), one at a time and only while the workers have fewer than two jobs in hand (`owDetailAsk`: the same workers make the chunks of ground round the player, which never wait for these), and kept (`WT.tileCap` grows past 1 km: about 2200 at 3 km; turned down, the far ones go at once). Such a tile is slimmed to its heights and water past 600 m (`owDetailSlim`: the grids its near chunk of ground is shaded from, about 220 KB, are what wterrain.js lets go once that chunk is built anyway, and the workers make them again when it is). A tile is made only once the roads of the villages round it are known (`wsReady`), which the game works out to 1.5 km: past that `owDetailRoads` asks for them - the villages' sites ring by ring of 720 m cells round the hero, one every other frame (one costs 10-100 ms; `wsWant` would make all the missing ones at once), then the roads of those in the rings done (`wsWant`, the world's own jobs, nearest first, 2 ms a frame more while there are any). `wsWant(r)` looks at the cells within r and, through `wsEdgesOf`, at the neighbours of their neighbours, so the rings reach two cells further than the roads asked for, and a tile is asked for only four rings inside them. A road is the same whenever it is made (the land and its two villages only), so the world is the same (tests/world-sig2.js matches). The stones are built at the world's own pace (`WRK.ms`, 3 ms a frame).
- **Measured (v55)** on an Intel HD 520, seed 1, Render distance 400, Detail distance 3000: over the meadows of the highlands by the start (-121, -373) the far grass is about 30 000 plants (130 000 triangles) in 27 draw calls, +2.4 ms of the GPU's time a frame; in the floating plateau (-1449, -2943) the full cliffs bring the stones to about a million triangles, +2.0 ms (of a frame of about 37 ms at these settings). The plateau's full cliffs fill in about 1600 frames.

## Trees (src/trees.js)
The deciduous trees of the streamed world, after the stylized low-poly references: round, faceted clumps of leaves on slender trunks and boughs that bend, every facet catching the light on its own (flat shading), darker under the crown and towards its middle, lighter on top; the canopy sways a little in the wind, the trunk does not.
- **The models** (`trwTemplates`, built once, the same in every world): five kinds in four variants each - **round** (a broadleaf: a trunk forking into two to four boughs under a dome of faceted balls), **oak** (a short thick trunk with a flared foot and roots, four or five long boughs each with a side branch, a wide crown), **tall** (an oval crown of stacked balls round a tall straight trunk), **umbr** (a slender trunk bent in an S, long rising branches ending in broad flat pads at different heights), **young** (a thin trunk and two to four small balls). Every tree in the world is 1.25 times its model (`TRW.SC`), scaled again 0.8-1.2 and turned at random. Four levels each: L0 near (clumps from the 80-facet ball, about 600-1700 triangles), L1 (the same clumps from the 20-facet ball: the crown keeps its outline, 240-500), L3 (the clumps merged into two to seven blobs that fill the outline, 60-250), L2 far (the three biggest clumps a fifth bigger over a three-sided trunk that reaches 2.5 m into the ground, so a far tree never floats where the coarse far terrain lies lower; 50-70).
- **Where they grow** (`trwPlace`): a level-0 tile (128 m) is 18 x 18 cells of 7.1 m with one try each, from the cell's own seeded stream (nothing is drawn from the world's stream), reading only the world's own functions - the forest overlay (`wgForest`), the region weights, the height with the streams' valleys carved (`wgH0` + `wgHydroApply`) - so a tile's trees are the same near or 3 km off, made first or last, and none pops in or out as the player walks. A cell holds a tree 1.4 % of the time in the open (lone trees) and up to 71 % deep in a forest (a tree every 8.5 m or so); a little fewer in the highlands and none high on their mountains, a little fewer on the plateau and in the elderglade, fewer as the ground steepens (none past a slope of .72 on the cell's corners, nor within 3 m of a drop, nor where the drawn ground under the trunk is steeper than 1 - its 2 m lattice sampled from the same function); none in water (a stream, a lake, a sinkhole's pool), within 5 m of a stream's channel (its ribbon and the ground's water band reach 2.2 m past it) or less than 1.2 m above the sea, none on the summit of a Great Elder mountain (fewer on its flanks). The kind: deep in a forest round, oak and tall trees with young ones and hardly an umbrella; young trees and umbrellas towards the edges; broad umbrellas and oaks alone in the open; the elderglade has more round and tall ones, the plateau more umbrellas; a slow field makes stands where round, oak or tall trees lead. Each tile is tinted by its region (the elderglade lusher, the highlands darker, the plateau warmer).
- **Kept clear** (`trwExclude`, looked at again whenever villages or roads are added): the keep (72 m) and its approach, every village's ground and 28 m round it, 6.5 m round every road, and the line where a stone ring or an arch stands. The villages round a tile (and the rings) are worked out before its trees are kept or dropped - one village a frame, as one costs 5-60 ms - so no tree ever stands where a village appears a moment later, whatever was loaded first. The game works out roads within 1.5 km of the player: a tile within 1.2 km of the player waits until the roads round it are known (from what is cached; it never makes them), so the trees near the player are the same on every visit; a far tile uses the roads known so far (a far tree on a road not yet worked out goes when the road is made, a kilometre or more off), and one whose trees were kept that way is held back - not shown, no trunks - once it comes within 1.2 km, until its roads are known and it has been looked at again.
- **Drawn** (`trwView`, every frame from `owView`): every tile within the end of the air (`owFogEnd`) has one merged mesh - L1 within `TRW.RA` (120 m), L3 within `TRW.R1` (320 m), beyond that L2 in its far class (all the trees out to 700 m, 60 % of them a fifth bigger out to 1.3 km, 36 % of them 45 % bigger beyond, so the land stays as wooded) - built nearest first, a few milliseconds a frame; a tile keeps its level for 32 m past an edge. The trees within `TRW.R0` (60 m) of the eye are the near ones, instanced (a mesh a template), gathered whenever the eye has moved 4 m. The hand-over is per tree in the shaders: a near instance draws only within R0, the same tree in its tile's mesh only beyond it, so none is drawn twice or not at all. The Foliage setting moves the bands (low / medium / high / ultra: near 45 / 55 / 60 / 70 m, L3 out to 220 / 270 / 320 / 480 m, the far classes keeping .6 / .8 / 1 / 1 of their part).
- **Shadows:** the near trees within `TRW.RS` (40 m) cast their own (the sharp near cascade), every other tree casts through its tile's mesh (the middle tiles within the far cascade's reach, `OW.sd`, layer 2): the shadow hand-over is at RS, so every tree casts once.
- **Collision:** the trunks of the trees within 240 m of the player are props (`OW.props` / `OW.propGrid`, type `tree`, r the trunk's radius): the hero cannot stand in one (`owCanStand`: `trwBlock`, with the hero's 0.28 m), villagers walk round them (vilnav.js), the grass and the ground cover keep off them, and a tower built on one cuts it down (`owClearProps`: `trwCut`, for the rest of the visit).
- **Cost**, measured on an Intel HD 520 in seed 1 at the default render distance (the GPU's time a frame, trees shown and hidden alternately, medians of five): +3 to +7.5 ms over a wooded hillside, at a forest edge and in the open by the keep (230 000-320 000 triangles), +9.5 ms deep in a dense forest (about 660 000), +9.3 ms with the render distance at its far end (2.8 km of air; 35 MB of meshes, all there after about 700 frames). Running at 6 m/s the frame's own work goes from 1.4 to 1.9 ms in the middle (99th percentile 15.7 -> 16.6 ms; the trees' part 0.4 ms on average, 8.5 ms at the most, when a dense tile is built) and the render from 4 to 6.6 ms. Placing a tile takes 3-4 ms, its mesh about 0.3 ms; about 20 MB of meshes for some 20 000 trees at the default render distance.
- **Checks:** `tests/trees-check.js` (after the world is set up and the player has stood somewhere): the same placement again and in another order, nothing by the keep, on a village's ground, on a road, under a ring or an arch, in water (against the level-0 tiles' own water) or on drawn ground steeper than the trunk allows, the height within 1.2 m of the drawn ground, the trunks blocking the hero, the near trees exactly those within R0 + 14 m, the villages round every tile with trees known, every tile shown within 1.2 km of the player kept with its roads known (the rest held back, showing nothing), no trunk prop twice. Seed 1, nine places (three on the plateau, the keep, a village, a forest, a river, the elderglade, the highlands; 16 000-27 000 trees each): 144 checks, 0 failed.

## Camera and controls
On a PC the hero has three cameras and V cycles them: third person -> free camera -> first person -> third person (`camMode`, `camSet`, `camCycle` in `src/kingsgaard.html`). Third person is the orbit camera (its boom is shortened by any building between the hero and the camera, `tpcBoom`); the free camera is the existing WASD / Space / Shift one; first person puts the camera at the hero's eye and hides the hero's model (`H.mdl`; the group stays in the scene for everything else). G (and the middle button) switch the mouse between Free Mouse and Free Look; V and G wait while a window is open; a change of camera never drops the pointer lock (`lookSync`). F opens and closes doors (see the interiors README). The cutaway of the house the hero is in is used only in third person. Mobile is unchanged (the doors open by themselves).

**No waves, hearts, gold or gems; the hotbar.** Kingsgaard is an RPG: the tower-defence systems it grew from are gone from the open world - no waves (the Start-wave and AUTO buttons are hidden, nothing calls a wave), no hearts (an enemy that reaches the keep costs nothing, `leak`), no gold or gems (their counters are hidden, no rewards are given or shown, `floatGold`/`addGems`), no building (Build is hidden). The old code and the saved fields stay, so nothing that names them breaks and old saves load. In place of the wave button is a hotbar of six slots (`hotRender`, `hotSelect`, `hotSet`, `hotGet`, `hotSelected`; saved in `meta.hot` {sel, slots}): keys 1-6 (or the number pad) or a click choose a slot, the chosen one is lit and raised, a `hotbar` event goes out on `window` when it changes; a slot holds an item {id, name, icon: an SVG string} - none yet. Keys 1-6 are the hotbar's: the scroll key binds default to 7, 8, 9 and the other slots are free to bind to any key that is not taken (Settings, Key binds); a bind on 1-6 from an older save is dropped. The control hints moved to the top left.

**Walking, running, sprinting.** W (with A, S, D) walks, Shift runs, Alt sprints (`tpcPace`, `TPC_PACE` in `src/kingsgaard.html`: 1, 3.6 and 6.2 world units a second for a hero about 1.17 tall - an easy walk, a run and a sprint, the speeds of a person, so each gait looks as it should; C no longer toggles walking, walking is the default). The body has weight: the speed is a critically damped spring (`TPC_ACC`, `H.tpV`/`H.tpA`), so from standing it takes about a second to reach a walk (a weight shift, a short first step, then the stride), longer to wind up to a sprint, and it eases to a stop (about 0.6 s from a walk, 1.2 s from a sprint); the way it goes turns along a curve (`H.tpD`, `TPC_TURN`: quickly on the spot, wider the faster it goes) and a sharp turn slows it first; a knock-out or not being steered clears the speed. While you walk the hero, Alt is the sprint and not a modifier: keys pressed with it still work, and the browser's own Alt shortcuts (Alt+D, Alt+F, ...) are held back (`altMod`; Alt cannot be bound to a scroll). The controller (`pcanim.js`) has three gaits (`PCG`), chosen by speed and blended while it changes: the walk (always a foot on the ground, heel strike and roll onto the toes, the body vaulting over the leg, the arms swinging loosely), the run (a flight between steps, the heel kicked up behind, the knee driven forward, landing under the body on the middle of the foot, elbows bent, fists) and the sprint (a short contact on the ball of the foot, the heel up to the seat, the knee up towards the hip, a strong lean, the arms pumping). The feet match the ground the body covers: the strides a second rise with the speed and the stride is what the speed leaves; a foot on the ground is held where it was put, in the world (its heel and ball along its own direction), so it cannot slide whatever the body does - speeding up, slowing down, turning, a wall; a foot in the air goes on a curve against the hip from where it left the ground to where it lands, ahead of the hip along the way the body goes; the walk vaults over a nearly straight leg (the reach is taken at 99.65% of the leg, a knee of about 9 degrees; the shoulders turn against the hips, the hips drop on the free side, the body slows a little as it rises over the leg and gains as it falls onto the next, `surge`); where a leg cannot reach its foot the pelvis comes down - smoothly (a soft minimum of the two legs and a stiff spring, never more than the legs' slack above what they need), and a leg stretched to its length behind leaves the ground instead of pulling the pelvis after it; a foot in the air counts only as it comes down to land. The body leads and the legs follow under it: the upper body leans forward with the gait (a little in the walk, more in the run, most in the sprint) and the pelvis is carried forward over the feet (`lead`), so a foot lands under the body and pushes back behind it instead of walking out in front; it leans further into speeding up and back when it stops; a walk getting going takes slower, shorter steps than its pace; the run lands under the body, its elbows open behind and its hands cross a little in front, and the spine gives under each landing. In a long skirt (less in a skirt or a long coat) the run and the sprint take shorter, quicker strides with less knee drive, as anyone does. No two strides are alike (`pcVarInit`, `pcVarStep`): a figure has its own way of moving, a mood over it that changes every 4-10 s (the arm swing, how bent the elbows, how high the arms are carried and how far out, the foot height, the lean and the stoop, how much the hips and the chest turn, the bob, the cadence, a lean to one side, a tilt of the head), a little change with every stride and a slight difference between the arms; while walking the head now and then glances aside or down at the way (less and more rarely in a run, never in a sprint, a fight or standing still). All of it eased, and the feet still keep to the ground (the cadence changes the stride). Villagers walk each in their own way too (`vilWalk`: cadence, arm swing, foot height and bob drawn once a villager, never from the world's stream). `tests/gait-lab.js` drives the hero with the keys frame by frame: filmstrips of a stride (`__gl.strip`), single frames (`__gl.snap`) and how far a planted foot's contact point moves in the world (`__gl.slip`: 0 at all three paces, straight and turning).

## Aiming
All hero-cast spells aim with the camera: a small dot at the middle of the canvas, a ray from the camera through it (terrain, buildings, walls and enemies; the hero is skipped), a projectile or effect from the hero's casting origin to what the ray found, and a range fallback in the sky. `src/aim.js` holds the rays, the spell categories (bolt, slash, ground, self, summon: `AIM_CAT`) and the ground lift of area effects on slopes. Tests: `aim-spells.js`, `aim-camera.js`, `aim-modes.js` (the same casts in all three cameras).

## Collision
Walking is one rule (`src/interior/walk.js`: `owiValid`, `owiResolve`, `owiMove`): a position is valid when no exact box of a loaded building overlaps the hero's circle and the terrain grid allows it; a step is resolved against the boxes and the grid together, and if the hero ever finds itself on an invalid spot a small recovery (`owiRecover`, at most 4 m, never into another collider, never repeated) glides it out. The foundation ledge, the steps' cheek walls and the porch posts belong to the ground floor only (`OWI_GND`), because the upper floors of a jettied house reach out over them. Tests: `hit-trap.js`, `hit-stress.js`, `hit-stairs.js`, `hit-recover.js`.

## Sun, sky and shadows
One cycle drives everything: `dayStep` turns `meta.dayT` (20 minutes of day, 10 of night) into `DAYC`. The sun rises in the east (+x), is highest in the south (+z) at midday (about 62 degrees) and sets in the west (-x); its height never falls below about 7 degrees, where its light is already faint and red, so shadows are about 35 m long for a 10 m wall at sunrise and 5 m at noon (`DAYC.dir`, `DAYC.el`). The moon takes the same path at night and the swap happens where the light is almost out, so the change of direction is not seen. The sky (`owSkyInit` / `owSkyDome`) is a gradient from the fog colour (so far land melts into it) to a deeper zenith with a warm glow and a small sun disc in the direction of the light, drawn first at the far plane like the stars and the moon: terrain and buildings cover it. The light colour is neutral at noon and warm low in the sky; the ambient light is cooler as daylight fades.
Shadows: the sun's own shadow map is the sharp near cascade (4096 on a PC, a box of 55-150 m round the camera, snapped to its texel grid), a second light renders the far cascade (the Shadow Distance setting: the box is that wide, 150 to 1000 m) from static casters only and is refreshed when the sun has turned a degree, the camera has moved a fifth of the distance or 6 s have passed. The sun's light stands 110 m from its target in the open world (it was 17 m, which left tall casters outside the shadow box); the bias grows as the sun gets low. `tests/sun-test.js` samples the whole cycle.

## Grounding
Every prop stands on the lowest ground under its footprint (`Yf` in `owTownDress`) and poles go 15-22 cm into it; fence posts stand at both ends of every piece and rails are pitched along the slope (`kitBuilder().PY`); each pole of a washing line is in the ground at its own height; lamp posts have a buried foot; garden beds follow the lowest corner; five furniture pieces that floated or sank by 3-8 cm (pallet, plank stack, cradle, sacks, firepit) were redrawn. `tests/ground-items.js` measures the lowest point of every furniture piece, `ground-lod.js` the distance between the height function the props were placed with and each level of detail of the visible terrain (at most 5 cm up to 340 m, 25 cm to 700 m).

## Settlements
Tier 4 towns are no longer a grid in a circle. The shape is one of five (`v.orgShape`: linear, cross, cluster, compact, terrain) chosen from the seed: the main street is longer or shorter, lanes leave it where the ground allows, bend, sometimes fork and end in small squares or in gates (`owOrgStreets`); houses stand along the streets (rows near the centre, detached houses with fenced yards further out), and the town's inside is the neighbourhood of its streets (`insideOrg`). The wooden fence is built from yard boundaries and gate runs (`fenceEdge` never builds across a road or a building), with two posts and a bar at every exit; the circular palisade remains only for Tier 5 cities without walls. Which kind of house a plot gets depends on how central it is (`cenOf`: distance to the square and the main street, with slow noise; `resAt`): Tier 4 towns mix `rowh` (Tier 4) with `rowt` (Tier 3 terrace houses, new) towards the edge; Tier 5 cities use `rowc` in the centre, `rowh` between and `rowt` at the edge. No hut or cottage is ever built in a Tier 4 or Tier 5 settlement, so Tier 3 is the lowest. `tests/settle-test.js` checks the tiers, the shape and that every building front connects to the square.
Saves carry `g: 2`; the buildings of a world generated before this change are laid out differently, so a save without it restores the village flags but not the per-building state.

## Heroes
The walking hero is drawn 19% larger than at first (`HS` 2.85: 2.4, then 2.6, now 2.85; 1.26 m against doors of 1.8 m), villagers 10% larger (2.53, with -4%..+4% from a hash of the place); its collision radius, camera distances and spell origins are as before (the origin and the first-person eye follow the model's height).

## Villagers
The people of the villages are made of the player's character's own parts (`src/villager.js`), so the two look like people of one world: the same low-poly heads, body sculpt, garments, hair, hats and flat shading, at the same size. The hero's builder is far too heavy for dozens of figures (about forty meshes on some forty bones, 4,600 to 8,200 triangles without the kit, 15-70 ms a figure), so it only makes twelve templates (`VIL_T`: a gender, a head, a hairstyle, a base layer, perhaps an outer layer, bottoms and shoes; four of them with the wide hat) and each template is baked once into one small skinned mesh: every visible part of the figure in the creator's pose (arms a little out, elbows soft), skinned on the CPU (`vilParts`), the weights of the hero's bones added up into the villager's seven - body, legL, legR, armL, armR and the knees shinL, shinR, at the hero's hips, knees and shoulders - so hips, knees and shoulders bend softly, and hair and hat stay on the head. What hangs from the hips on the hero's springs (a skirt, the skirts of a tunic or a coat, a cloak: the chains `sk`, `tu`, `ct`, `cl`) gives a share to the nearer leg - the thigh from the hip down, the shin too below the knee (`VIL_HANG`) - so a villager's legs never come through a skirt. They walk as the hero walks (`vilWalk`): the phase goes with the ground covered, the foot that carries moves back under the hip exactly as fast as the villager goes on, the knee bends by two-bone IK in the walk's plane, the body dips where a leg must reach. Colour: the template is built twice with marker colours in the six slots of the look (skin, hair, base layer, outer layer, bottoms, shoes) and every part's colour is solved as a mix of the slots and a fixed colour (`vilSolve`: pcDark, pcLite and pcMix are all such mixes; the felt of the wide hat is a seventh slot), so a villager gets its own colours from one small colour buffer (`vilColors`) while position, normal, weights and index are shared by every villager of its template. The baked figure is welded and reduced (`vilDecimate`: quadric edge collapse onto existing points, so every point is a point of the hero's sculpt with its weights and colour; an edge never joins two parts, open edges are held, a collapse that would turn a face over or pinch the surface is refused, and the error of a small part counts more, so faces, hands and eyes keep their detail) to 3,200 triangles, and on to 700-860 for the far figure (24 m and more from the camera, `VIL_FAR`: the same points with an index of its own), both under a `THREE.LOD` on the one skeleton. One material for all of them (`vilMat`, program key `pcrimvil`): the hero's low-poly shading (`pcRimPatch`: wrapped light, the sky light bent up, the soft rim) with a little of the vertex colour as light; one draw call a villager (five before). The world's stream is drawn exactly as before (the colour of the top, the hat, the spot, the two offsets, the wait, the phase), so worlds and saves are the same (`tests/vil-ws.js`: world-sig of 24 seeds equal to the baseline); gender, template, skin (never too close to the colour of the top), hair colour, outer layer, bottoms and shoes come from a hash of the place. Scale (2.78, -4 % to +4 %), behaviour and animation are unchanged; the plank a villager carries lies across the hands of its template.
The templates are baked while the main menu waits (`vilWarm`: in the browser's idle time, one stage of 5-56 ms at a time, only while the main menu is shown); what is not made yet is made when the first realm is forged (60-150 ms a template, 0.7-1.5 s for all twelve, once a session); making the 128 villagers of a world then takes about 40 ms. Cost of drawing (seed 3, Hartwick; frame times with `gl.finish`, the 10th/25th percentile of 150-200 frames, new, old and no villagers in turns): 64 villagers in view cost 0.9 ms by day and 1.8 ms by night (the old box figures 2.5 and 3.3 ms); on a busy square with all sixteen in view, near and far, 918k triangles and 460 draw calls against 884k and 524 with the old figures, and the frame no slower than with them (25.6/31.6 ms against 28.5/35.4 ms by day, 33.3/42.2 against 37.9/43.4 by night; 25.4/30.1 and 31.9/36.6 with none). `tests/vil-lab.js` puts the templates in a row on open ground of the world (`__vl.sheet`: pictures from the front, the sides, the back and three quarters, by day and night, walking, carrying, working; `__vl.cmp`: a template at full detail beside the baked one; `__vl.hero`: the hero beside them); `tests/vil-perf.js` measures.

## The walk: the body over its feet (v34)
The walk was all in front of the body: a stretched leg left the ground early from 55% of its stance (PCG tof), and with the pelvis high the leg was straight right under the hip, so every foot landed 0.38 leg lengths ahead and lifted directly under the hip - the feet never got behind the body. Now the walk keeps its foot down to 88% of the stance (tof .88; run and sprint keep .55), lands a little nearer (ahead .42), rolls the heel up from mid-stance (roll .5, pOff -.65: the ankle rises, so the leg stays on the ground behind without the pelvis dropping), with rounder swing curves (k0/k1 .32) and bob .016. Measured (seeded): feet land 0.30 ahead of the hip and leave 0.41 behind (were 0.38 and 0.00), pelvis bob 0.05 leg lengths, pelvis jolt (max second difference) 4.0e-3 against 5.6e-3, swing knee 61 degrees (was 74); foot slip 0 at every pace and turning. Villagers the same way (VILW: ah .42, heel roll from .5 to .6 rad, cadence x1.15 - they are shorter than the hero and walk as fast -, hip sink .004, and the hips' drop is now a soft maximum of both legs with the landing foot counting as it comes down, followed by a critically damped spring): land 0.32 / leave 0.52 (were 0.48 / 0.53), bob 0.053 (was 0.128), jolt 0.0057 (was 0.083), planted-foot drift 0.5 mm.

The walk's arms (v36): a smaller swing that stays by the body - PCG.walk arm .26 (was .4), armF .06 (carried a little behind), elF -.1 (the elbow bends less coming forward, was -.25), armX .015 (less across the body, was .04); and in the walk the variety widens the swing half as much and hardly carries it forward (run and sprint keep their variety). Measured over five seeded walks: the hands reach at most 0.19-0.26 leg lengths ahead of the shoulder (were 0.35-0.43), 0.19-0.28 behind; whole swing 0.42-0.51 (was 0.53-0.81); foot slip 0.

## Doors upstairs, lamps up close (v34)
The front door's colliders (the doorway while shut or moving, the open leaf) had the mask OWI_ALL - every floor - so on the floor above (where a jettied upper storey runs over the door) there was an invisible wall above the door. They are on the ground floor only now (OWI_GND; tests/door-flow.js 36/36, with a check for it). Lamps: the nearest three lanterns get a light with a shadow map each, the next posts one shared light per post; a lantern handed from its post's shared light to a shadow light of its own went dark (the shared light faded out at once, the shadow light waited for its shadow map and then faded in). Now a lantern a shadow light is taking over stays in its post's shared light, which gives it what the shadow light does not yet give (computed every frame from the shadow lights' fade), and a shadow light that is let go stays on (up to 1.2 s) until the shared light that takes its lantern over is on. Measured walking up to six lamps of Hartwick at midnight: v33 fell to 1-17% of the light right next to the lamp before it came back; now it never falls on the way in.

## Standing on what is there (feet on stones, steps and slopes)
The paving stones of streets, paths and squares stand 4-5 cm proud of the terrain, and everyone used to stand at terrain height, so shoes sank into every paved street. While roadstones.js lays a tile's stones it rasterises their tops into a 20 cm grid (`t.sh`, a 1.2 m margin round the tile; dropped with the stone mesh), and `owWalkY(x,z)` - the height one stands at out of doors - is the terrain or the highest stone top in the four cells round the point (a joint between stones is bridged). Checked against the drawn stones by rays: median 0.3 mm, at most 2.6 cm higher at a chamfered edge. Dirt paths and earth squares (v35): the ground tiles are drawn 4 cm over the terrain (so they do not flicker in it), so `owWalkY` also takes the tile's height where the tile is drawn - interpolated over the tile's own triangles (the same diagonals as the mesh) and weighted by how much of it the ground shader draws there (its alpha from aE.x: fully inside, fading over the 0.7 m of its edge), never below the terrain (`t.gh` keeps the tile's positions and aE; dropped with the tile's mesh). By rays: the walking height equals the drawn path (median 0.0 mm); planted feet on a dirt path never below it (hero worst 0.0 mm, villagers worst 6 mm; before, 4 cm in). The hero (`owiHeroY`) and the villagers stand on it. Each foot also finds the ground under itself: the hero's (pcanim.js, `t.ground` = `owFootY`: in the building the hero is in, `owiFloorY` gives the floor, stairs, ledge and treads at any point, else `owWalkY`) and the villagers' (vilAnim, `o.ground`: `owWalkY`, or the treads of the house on a door visit), sampled under the heel, the ankle and the ball and the highest taken (a foot across the edge of a step stands on the higher tread); the foot is raised or lowered from the ground under the body and the pelvis comes down by the legs' reach. Measured: the hero's planted feet on stones median 0 mm (worst 2.7 mm; before, 4.5 cm in), on house steps never below the tread (before up to a whole step); villagers on the ground median 3 mm above, no step over 2 cm in; on door steps 0.4-1.2% of steps more than 2 cm in. Hero foot slide unchanged (0). The hero is drawn at HS 3.72 (1.65 m, 92% of a 1.8 m door; it was 3.45); the villagers keep 3.45 (VIL_HS); the collision radius is unchanged.

## How villagers walk (vilAnim)
Thirteen bones per villager, baked from the hero's own skeleton (`VIL_BMAP`: hips, thighs, shins, feet, chest - spine, chest and collarbones - head, upper arms, forearms; a bone not named goes with its nearest named parent, so hair and hats follow the head). The walk is the hero's walk at a villager's budget: the cadence rises with speed (the first steps from standing slower); a foot is down 60% of the stride and is held where it was put in the world (it is re-planted only if the villager is moved by something else), so it does not slide when the villager speeds up, slows down or turns (planted-foot drift in a village: median 0.0009 straight, 0.0018 turning, world units); the foot lands on the heel, rolls flat, lifts the heel and rolls over the ball, toes up in the swing; the thighs undo the hips' sway, drop and turn (rotation order ZYX) so the hips move over still feet. The hips bob, shift and drop; the chest turns against them, leans a little forward (more when speeding up) and breathes; the head stays level, looks where the way turns, glances aside now and then and looks at the hero within about 5 m. The arms swing against the legs a little behind them, the elbows bending as they come forward. The speed itself is a critically damped spring (`vilAcc`: half pace in 0.5 s, 90% in 1.2 s; slows before a stop). Each villager has its own pace (0.86-1.12 w/s), cadence, arm swing, step height, bob, hip turn, stoop, elbows and head carriage (`vilVk`, Math.random - never the world's stream). Carrying a plank: arms forward, elbows bent (the plank sits on the chest bone); repairing: hammering with the elbow, head down. Cost: about 0.007 ms a villager a frame.

**v37 - a calmer walk, easing in and out.** The bob was mostly the hips dipping to reach the feet, not the bounce itself: a foot landed far ahead (`VILW.ah` .42 -> .38) and the foot behind kept pulling the hips down until it lifted. Now the foot behind lets go late in its stance (`VILW.tf` .75: from 75% of the stance its pull fades, as it rolls onto its toes - the hero's early toe-off), the extra bounce is smaller (`bob` .012 -> .005), the cadence lower (`cad` 1.15 -> 1.04), the pace a stroll (`vilVk().spd` .72-.92 m/s, was .86-1.12), and the arms swing less, next to the body. Head height while walking moves 1.2-1.5% of its height on flat ground (was 2.2-4.2%). Starting: the speed is a critically damped spring (`vilAcc`, 2.8/s up, 4/s down): half pace after ~.6 s, 90% after ~1.4 s. Stopping: never harder than an even, gentle deceleration (`vilStopCap`, `VILN.DEC` .4 m/s^2) over the ground that is left, so the walk eases out from ~.8 m before the spot and ends on it (arrival at 1.5 cm, not 6 cm with a jump). Nav fixes found while testing it: the line between two map cells is checked wherever anything solid is within .75 m of either (`vilOpen`; before, only near fences - a house corner could stand on the line between two 'walkable' cells); line checks sample finely enough that nothing reaches into the 20 cm walking circle between two samples; a waypoint counts as reached at 2 cm (was 10 cm: the next leg started beside the checked line); a villager walled in has its speed set to what it covers (it starts gently again), tries a new way after .4 s, and stops short when someone stands on the last 45 cm of its way; getting closer to the end counts as headway.

## Villagers find their way (src/vilnav.js)
Villagers walk round everything solid and use the doors of the houses. What is solid: the occupancy grid holds most of it, but coarsely (a lamp post or a barrel can stand in a cell it calls free, a fence is only its posts), so while a village is built it also records the solid things themselves (`vilSolid`: the footprint of every building - row houses +28 cm, the others +60 cm at the sides, which covers every collider box of the 290 planned buildings of seed 3 - the steps up to every door, fences, gate posts, palisades, gardens, barrels and crates, the town's props and lamps, haystacks, fields). Recording only: the grid and the world's stream are untouched (world-sig 24/24). A villager stands only where its circle is clear of those, of the grid's solid cells and of trees and rocks (`vilFree`).
The way: A* over the grid's cells round the village (8 neighbours, a diagonal only past two free cells, a small cost beside walls); a cell is walkable when a circle of 30 cm at its centre is free, and where something thin (a fence, a post, a garden bed) lies near, the step between two cells is checked too (`vilEdge`: a fence can run between two free cells). The map is built a few ms a frame when a village comes near (`vilNavFill`: the solids, the trees, every cell, the edges, then the regions - pockets with no way out, such as a fenced yard, are never used); the villagers wait unseen meanwhile (1-2 s for the biggest city). The path is then pulled straight where the way is clear (runs of up to 12 m). At most one search per village per frame, capped at 4 ms (typically 0.3-1 ms); in a big town villagers pick places within about 30 m.
On the way each step must land on free ground (else it slides, then asks for a new way); a corner is only left behind once the next one is in sight; others ahead slow a villager and it steps round them on the side away from them; villagers standing on one another step apart; the hero pushes a villager aside (it slides along a wall if it must) and one with nowhere to go stops the hero like a post. A villager that makes no headway for a while asks again, then does something else; one ever found inside something solid is put on the nearest free spot.
Doors: about three times in ten a villager visits a house near the hero (one whose interior plan the game has made already - making a plan costs 20-40 ms, so villagers never make one): it walks to the front, climbs the steps (the hero's step heights), opens the door (it swings in, as the hero's does), walks in to a free spot clear of furniture, stairs and the swing of the leaf, shuts the door behind it if it opened it, stays 6-22 s (seen through the windows, and inside when the hero comes in), opens the door, comes out and shuts it again from the foot of the steps (retried while the hero stands in the doorway). A door the hero opened is left open; a door is never shut on a villager (`owdSet` asks `vilInDoor`: "Someone is in the way"); a villager waits for the hero in the way, and after a few seconds stays where it is (inside) or turns back (outside). One villager per house at a time.
Cost (seed 3, 90 s of every village): 0.05-0.37 ms a frame per village on average, worst frames 2-15 ms (map stages, a long search).

## Villagers at home: a bed each, home at nightfall (src/vilhome.js, v38)
Every villager has a bed in its own village and goes to it when night falls: to its front door along the village's ways, in, shuts the door, through the rooms and up the stairs, sits on the bed, lies back under a quilt and sleeps till morning; then it gets up, goes down and out, and its day goes on. Each night a tenth of a village's villagers stay up and wander the streets instead (a different few each night; they call on no houses at night).
The clock: the day is 1800 s (DAYC), the sun sets at 1200. Each villager goes to bed at its own time between 1110 and 1210 (`VILH.BED`) and gets up between 40 and 160 the next morning (`VILH.WAKE`), from a hash of the village and the villager. Who stays up: `floor(n*.1 + a hash of the night)` of the n villagers, picked by a hash of the night (`vilHWanderer`).
The beds: given out a little at a time when a village comes near (`vilHAssign`): each villager, in the order they were made, takes a free bed in the house nearest the place it was made at (`hx/hz`, kept on the villager by owMakeVillager - no draw from the world's stream). A single bed or pallet is one place, a double bed two (couples). Only beds a villager can reach are given out (`vilHReach`: the floor cells reachable from inside the front door, and up each flight whose foot is reached). Where a hamlet has more people than beds, the rest get a bedroll on the floor of the nearest house that is lived in (`vilHFloor`: a strip 1.8 x .7 m clear of every collider, out of the way in from the door; at most two a house; a storehouse as the last resort); the bedroll is laid out while its sleeper lies on it. The same beds every time the world is loaded. Seeds 3, 11, 7: 389 villagers, all with a bed they can reach (216 upstairs, 8 bedrolls).
Making a house's plan costs 3-60 ms (a three-storey town house): by day the beds are given out only from houses the game has planned anyway (round the hero); from 110 s before the first bedtime and through the night the rest are planned here, one a quarter second at most and none while the frames are slow. The beds come out the same either way (the order of the villagers and the houses decides). A village that was not being run (just come near, or the game loaded at night) puts its sleepers straight into bed once they have beds; until then they wait unseen.
Inside (`vilHRoute`): a grid per floor (12 cm) of where a villager's circle (21 cm) fits, the house's collider boxes laid on it (rasterised, not every cell asking every box); breadth-first, eight ways, a corner only past two free cells, pulled straight; the flights are climbed as the hero climbs them (from 45 cm before the foot to 45 cm past the top), the level changing half way up (`vilHLv`). The bed is reached from its side at the hips (its own side of a double bed), the other side or the foot - whichever the way reaches. The floor the villager is on gives its height and its feet (owiFloorY).
Lying down (`vilHPose`, three poses blended): standing at the side facing the bed -> sitting on it, hips on the mattress, legs along it, facing its foot -> lying back, the hips staying put, head on the pillow, shoulders propped, toes along the bed; getting up is the same backwards (about 2 s). The quilt (`vilHCover`) is in the bed's own blanket's colour (it.bc, kept by furniture3.js when the bed is drawn) and is made with the house's furniture material - the light grid shader finds the light from the vertices' own positions in the house's frame (lightmap.js `vLp = position`), so the quilt is built where it lies, not moved there. The mattress height comes from the kind of bed drawn (it.bv).
Two of a house going out (or in) one after the other: a step through the doorway with the door shut opens it first (`door:1` on those steps - the first had shut it after the second found it open). Drawn or not: a villager in a house whose rooms are not built (far off) is not drawn; on a floor above the hero's in the house the hero is in (the cutaway) neither. A door far from the hero and the camera (`VILH.FAR` 75 m) is not swung: nobody sees it, and owdTick would shut it again. One asleep on a bedroll stops the hero like a post (a bed's own collider does that for a bed); sleepers are not pushed aside.
The furnishing changed for it: most cottages had no bed (the hall's bed was placed last, after the table and the rest, and found no room) - now it is placed right after the hearth; huts sleep on straw pallets (`owiPallet`: long side to the wall, the way in kept as the doorway and 15 cm either side, 12 cm from other pieces), two where the hut has room; the pallet is .8 m wide (was .9); a double bed's second pillow lies on the first at the head (it lay half a metre down the bed).
Tests: `tests/vil-home.js` (__vh.beds(vi): every villager's place and whether the way to it exists; __vh.night(vi, o, cb): a village from dusk to mid-morning with the clock running - in bed at 1330, the wanderers up, everyone out by 300, a villager walking through a collider of its floor, a height off its floor, NaN, failed routes, ms).

## Stairs, steps and the floor the hero is on
A flight belongs to the two floors it joins (`f.from`, `f.to`, an `id`), has a mesh of its own (`ic.flights`, geo.js) and is drawn only while the hero is on one of those floors (`owiStairVis`, called from `owiHeroY` whenever the floor changes): on floor 1 of a house with three storeys you see the flight that goes up from it and the one that arrives from below, never the flight above that (the cutaway used to clip the slab above and leave that flight floating). A house the hero is not in draws all its flights. The flight being walked on touches both floors, so it is never hidden. Collision is level-masked and unchanged.
The entrance steps of an enterable house (`b.meshes.s`) are one of the layers merged into the village block (batch.js). Entering the zone of the house, an open or moving door, or damage switch the house over to its own meshes; `owBlockUse` turned the layers off when merging but nothing turned `s` on again, so the steps vanished as the hero walked up to them. It does now (`tests/ext-stairs.js` fails on the old code).

## Furnishing, furniture, props, shadows
Rooms that the primary furnishing left with no piece (one-metre bedrooms on the upper floors of a row house, landings) get a fallback set (`interior/fallback.js`): chosen by the purpose of the room, its size and the tier of the house from its own stream `P.RF`, thin pieces only in narrow rooms, undone by the proof of walkability. `tests/empty-rooms.js`: 300 empty and 228 bare rooms in a sample world became 10 (the passages, empty by design) and 0.
Wardrobes (plank or panelled, one may stand open), tables (trestle, inn, bedroom, work, round, carved: chosen by `it.bk` / `it.rk`, the kind of building and room), fireplaces (courses of stone or brick, lintel, mantel, sooted firebox, andirons, logs, crane and pot, breast narrowing to the ceiling) and the stove are in `interior/furniture4.js`.
The outdoor prop library is `src/props.js`: stave barrels, board crates, buckets, baskets, sacks, bales, benches, troughs, carts, wheelbarrows, boards, signposts, bollards, planters, wash lines, anvils, chopping blocks, firewood, pottery, bins, tools and the Scroll Merchant's stands. Shapes vary with a hash of the place, not with the world's random stream, so the generation of the world is unchanged; `tests/prop-sheet.js`, `prop-ground.js`, `prop-world.js`.
Shadows: the furniture is a mesh of its own that casts (layer `c`, only for the house the hero is in), partitions cast, and the cutaway no longer clips shadows (`clipShadows` off), so the roof and upper storeys that are cut away for the eye still shade the room: sun reaches a floor through windows and doorways only (`tests/furn-shadows.js` reads the sun's shadow map back). ## Ceilings, roofs seen from inside, steps, pavilions
Every floor has a ceiling. The lower floors of a house have the slab of the floor above (stairwells cut out; better houses line it with plaster); the top floor used to have nothing, you looked into the underside of the roof. `owiBuildCeiling` (interior/geo.js) gives each room of the top floor its own ceiling from the plan (boards under exposed ties in a plain house, plaster in a better one), cut off at the outer walls and widened by half a partition so there is no gap over a partition. A roof slab is a box whose thickness hangs below the roof surface, so over the room near the eaves walls the roof comes down to t / cos(pitch) under the top of the walls (31 cm for shingle, 65 cm for thatch): under a gable roof the ceiling is flat in the middle and follows the underside of the roof near the eaves walls (a coved ceiling, `owiRoofShape`, `owiCeilAt`), under hipped and gambrel roofs it lies flat under the lowest point. Tall furniture is kept out of the low strips (`owiLowZones`), partitions of the top floor end under the ceiling, and a chimney stack now starts inside the roof (its foot used to hang under the ceiling). `tests/ceiling-test.js` shoots rays up from every room of 61 houses.
The cutaway has a clip plane for the roof of its own (`b.roofPlane`, below the lowest point of the roof): the plane of the storey is the ceiling of the hero's storey, which is the top of the walls, but the eaves hang up to 60 cm under that, so a rim of roof stayed round the cut-away house in 383 of 498 houses (`tests/roof-cut.js`).
Attached parts were built at floor height outside the foundation, half a metre above the ground beside the building (the tavern's bench and barrels, the storage house's crates, the carpenter's lumber, the stable's yard, the smith's forge lean-to, the annex and woodpile of a hut): they stand on decks and plinths in the foundation layer now (which is taken down to the ground where it falls away, `owDeepenFoundation`) or are props on the ground (props.js, `lumber`). `tests/attached-ground.js` shoots a ray under every such part: 78 buildings had parts hanging, none has now.
Sheds, stables and halls have steps up to a door that stays shut. The steps had no collision (the decorative steps `sm`). ## Saved worlds stay the same worlds
Since v42 (the streamed world) a save holds the seed and the state of each village by its key (`ow.gv`, `ow.vs`), and `tests/world-sig2.js` is the check: the regions, the keep, the village sites, heights, streams and lakes of 12 seeds (and with `build` the buildings of the villages built at the start) - compare with `tests/world-sig2-base.json` (the current generator, `WG_VER` 5, v48: seeds 1-6); `world-sig2-base-gv4.json` is that of v47, `-gv3` that of v46, `-gv2` that of v42-v45. `tests/eg-check.js` checks the elderglade (rings' variety, streams never rising and meeting the next, no walls beside them, no sheet of water between two levels, lakes apart, the regions' sizes). A change to the generator that changes the world raises `WG_VER`: saves of the older version keep the hero and lose only the state of their villages. What follows is about the old, fixed world (v41 and before).
A save holds the seed and, by index, the state of the villages and buildings; the world is generated again from the seed. All villages share one random stream, so a single changed draw in one village changes every village after it. A release has to generate the world its predecessor generated. What this release does for that: the stall plans exactly as the old stall (same numbers, same scale, no draws); the approach of solid buildings and pavilions is carved out of the occupancy grid when all villages are built (`OW.deferCarve`), not while they are built; the town dressing keeps the doors it always knew (pavilions are left out) and its clear-ground test is unchanged; a prop that would stand on the steps or the platform of a solid building or a pavilion is "placed" as it always was (the draws, the room it takes) but its parts are dropped and the room is given back at the end (`OW.ghostCells`); props new in this release are placed last (`late`). ## Windows
A window is a real opening in the wall (`owPanel`) with a four-bar frame, mullions, sills and shutters, and a pane in its own layer (`g`, `owFB`). The pane is transparent glass (`OW_GLASS`: 30% opacity, specular highlight, a sky reflection that grows at grazing angles, no depth write) while the interior behind it is loaded, and a dark opaque pane otherwise (`OW_GLASS_FAR`), because a hollow, back-face-culled shell would show through transparent glass. The wall itself is never transparent. Before this, the pane was a solid slab and the room behind it did not exist for any building that was not entered.

## Light in the houses (the light grid), and what keeps light out
The renderer is three.js r128 with a forward Phong pipeline and shadow maps; there is no ray tracing and no probe system, so the light of a room is a set of cheap, separate things that add up to something plausible. (1) The sun and the moon are the shadow-casting directional lights (above). A house that nobody is in casts through a plain proxy; the house the hero is in casts with its real meshes (`owiCastOn`): walls, roof, floors (`ic.slab`, one slab per storey, layer 3), the leaves of the front door and the furniture, so sun reaches a floor only through a window or an open door, and an upper floor is shaded by its own ceiling and the floor above. (2) The sky light (the hemisphere) is the same everywhere in the world, so inside a house it is scaled by a small BAKED grid (`src/interior/lightmap.js`): for every floor, 16 cm cells, two textures. The bake reads the plan only: how much of the light of each face of the house (front, back, left, right) gets to a cell through the windows (distance, angle, the partition walls in the way, a little spill through doorways), how much through the front door while it is open, how much the fires and lamps of the room reach (a point source lights what it can see, with a fall-off, not the room behind a wall), and ambient occlusion from the walls and the furniture (a cell near a wall or under a table is darker). Per frame only a few numbers change (the weight of each face from the sun's direction and the sky, the night, the flicker of the fires, how far the door is open: `owlmTick`), so a room with a window facing the sun is brighter than one facing away, a windowless room stays dark, a dark corner behind a wardrobe is darker than the middle of the floor, and opening the door brightens the hall behind it. The grid is sampled by the interior meshes and the walls of the house (`owlmPatch`, `USE_LM`), the warm light of fires and lamps is added in the same shader, and the street lamps and the other point lights of the world are switched off inside (a patch of the light chunk), so a lamp in the street does not light a room through its wall. (3) ONE real point light with a shadow map (`light.js`, `OWIL`) follows the nearest fire or lamp on the hero's floor (or the nearest street lamp out of doors at night): the map is only 512, drawn only when the source or the house changes, and it sees only the layer of the walls, floors, door leaves and furniture, so it does not light the floor above or the ground behind a wall. `tests/light-test.js` measures the picture (windowless rooms, sun against shade, fall-off, open door, AO, fire at night, the three leak checks), `leak-floor.js`, `sun-leak.js`.
Between the floors (`owlmThrough`): a solid floor stops light but a stairwell does not, so what a fire or a lamp on one floor puts into the opening of a flight of stairs comes out of the opening on the next floor (above and below), as a weaker, softer source that stands in the opening and falls off with the distance from it (`OWLM.thru` = 0.55, three quarters of the source's range, the partition walls of the floor it comes from in the way): the upper floor of a house with a fire downstairs is lit round the top of the stairs (as drawn, on the floor above a fire: 12 > 36 near the top of the stairs and 11 > 13 across the room, `tests/stair-light.js`) and not through its floor. Furniture stops the baked light of a fire: a wardrobe, a cupboard or a shelf (1.8 m) stops the light of a hearth (0.55 m) for the floor behind it, a table (0.9 m) does not stop the light of a lamp above it, a chair stops nothing that matters (`boxHit`); the real shadowed light does the same with its shadow map. The floor behind a tall piece, on the side away from the fire, is 2.5 to 3.7 times darker than the floor at the same distance from the fire in the open and never black (`tests/dark-corner.js`). Ambient occlusion at the walls is a little stronger (0.58 against 0.5 at the wall, the shader's floor 0.4 against 0.5): corners and crevices are darker, with no outline.
What it does not do: no bounce light (a bright patch of floor does not light the ceiling), no coloured bleeding, no soft shadow from the window itself, and the grid is baked from the plan, so a piece of furniture that is added later does not darken it until the building is loaded again. The leaks were closed at their sources: the sun through floors and ceilings (floors did not cast; now they do), the fire through floors (the point light saw every layer; now it sees only layer 3), street lamps through walls (they are off inside). The interior doorways of this game have no door leaves (only the front door opens and shuts), so light moves between rooms through a doorway, with the spill the grid gives it.
Far windows: a house whose interior is not loaded shows a dark pane with a sky reflection (`OW_GLASS_FAR`); a house somebody lives in glows in its windows after dark (`OW_GLASS_FARLIT`, `owiLitOf`), and the pane turns transparent over half a second when the interior comes (`owGlassAnim`) instead of switching at once.

## Paths on the ground
`owgBuildTile` builds, for every 32 m tile, a mesh of 0.8 m cells whose vertices carry what the surface is (the paving weights, the distance across the path, the half width, a code, the vector from the landmark) and a fragment shader (`OW_GGLSL`) draws cobbles, blocks, flagstones, ruts, pebbles and moss from the world position. Several roads and streets overlap in a city (roads from other villages run into the same gate), so at a vertex the surface is a blend of the paths that reach it, one entry per path (its nearest segment): the distance across and the direction are weighted by how much each covers the vertex, the wheel ruts fade out where the centre lines of two roads differ (they zigzagged from one road to the other before), the strongest paving and its style win over a dirt road beneath it, and the blocks of rectangular paving are laid by the distance along the path (`s0` per segment, `rid` per path, `owgPoly`), not by a direction vector that rotates between vertices (which drew hundreds of fine lines in a cell); where two paths with that paving meet or one doubles back, the pavement is cobbles. The cobble patches in a flagstone courtyard swap whole flagstones, so their edge follows the joints. `owgGateFit` (visual only): a drawn road that meets the wall of a city away from a gate, with a gate within 26 m, is bent through the gate over 28 m before it and 14 m after it; a road that goes in and out of the wall within 60 m is left alone (seed 5, Elmstead, still has one such crossing). `tests/road-wall.js`, `road-wall2.js` (the drawn roads), `path-net.js` (free ends: dead-end lanes are not pruned).

## City roads: paving stones
The streets, lanes and roads of the settlements where they are paved (the towns and cities of tier 4 and 5 and the heart of a tier-3 village; the small villages of tier 1 and 2 have earth streets, their paving is the cobbled ring of their square; owgPoly marks the segments `city`, owgSeg keeps every path's segments in order in `OW.G.paths`) are paved with real stones (`src/roadstones.js`) instead of the painted pattern: thousands of small convex slabs (about 0.38 by 0.23 m) with a flat top 2 cm above their bed, a little tilted and raised in the middle, a chamfer all round and a narrow joint to their neighbours; the bed under them (the ground tile, lowered by 2 cm there) is dark sand and mortar, so the joints read as shallow shadowed seams and the stone tops stand where the painted road was (feet stand on them as before). They are laid in courses across the road by the distance along the path and the distance from its middle, so the paving follows every bend and runs on through the joints of the segments; course depths, stone lengths and the offset of each course (a running bond) come from a hash of the path and the course, never from the world's stream, so the world and the saves are the same (world-sig 24/24). Every corner is moved a little, heights and tilts vary by millimetres, the colours are a restrained warm grey (`OWR.PAL`) varying by a few per cent, a few stones darker or warmer, worn edges a touch lighter; curbed paths have an edge course of long darker stones along each side. Where paths meet, one goes through and the other stops at its edge (a street before a road before a lane, then the wider, then the older path): the stones of the one that stops are cut along the edge of the other, a curbed path leaves its edge course out in the mouth of a path coming in across it (that path's stones fill it), and a path lying along another that goes through (the roads that run along the streets) counts only where it leaves it, so nothing overlaps and nothing is left open. The stones end at the squares (which keep their paving), in a clean course where the paving of a road ends, and round at a path's open end. Nothing else changes: the layout, the widths, the squares, the paths and alleys, the earth streets of the small villages, walking and collision (the stones are visual only); curbs and their edge courses are the towns' and cities' only.
Cost: one merged mesh per 32 m ground tile, built for the tiles within 64 m of the focus (`OWR.GEO`), a few milliseconds a frame, the nearest first (`owrBake` is a generator; a forced view builds at once); within 30 m (`OWR.NEAR`) every stone has its chamfer and raised middle (12 triangles for a rectangle), beyond that only its top (2), as index ranges of one geometry; drawn with the settlements' program (`owrMat`), right after the ground tiles (they do not write depth and are pulled forward, so drawn before them the stones were painted over at low angles), shadows received, not cast. A ground tile knows whether its stones are built (the sign of its `aSt`, `owrMark`): under built stones it is their bed, elsewhere it paints the same courses (`gSett`), so no street is ever bare. Where a path lies deep inside one that goes through, its stones are not even made. Hartwick (seed 3, the largest city): about 71,000 stones, 0.8 s of building for the whole city spread over frames (at most a few ms a frame while walking); a street view adds about 140,000 triangles and 7 to 10 draw calls, with frame times within the noise of the machine (`tests/road-perf.js`). `tests/road-lab.js` (`__rl.view` pictures with cameras of its own, `__rl.stats` stones and build times per settlement, `__rl.fig` a villager for scale). A path's stones end round at its own ends only within its width of the end (until upgrade 04 the round end cut every stone in the half plane beyond the end, and as the roads to the keep gate end pointing north, their paved stretches in the north half of the world had no stones, only the bare bed).
Every paved path and the paved squares (environment upgrade 06): the stones are laid on every paved path, a street, road, lane or footpath of any settlement (`owrStoneable`: paving 0.2 and up, not only the towns' streets); where the paving fades below 0.62 the stones thin out (the edges first) and weather, and their bed fades to the sand, so a paved road runs out into the sand instead of stopping at a line with painted paving beyond. The squares are paved in real stones after the designs the ground shader paints on them (`owqKind`): courtyards in flagstones (about 1.9 m) with patches of cobbles, the ring of cobbles round a village well thinning out to its edge, a town square's flagstone field framed by a band of blocks (1.15 by 0.56 m) with a ring of cobbles round its landmark, a city square's cobbles (about 0.54 m) framed by blocks with rings of big blocks and flagstones round its landmark. Cobbles and flagstones are the cells of a jittered grid (`owqCell`), blocks are rows in a running bond; each stone belongs to the region its seed lies in and is cut along that region's edge (`owqClip`), with the street stones' chamfer, a shade of its own and a little moss towards the square's edge. The ground under a paved square is the stones' bed (`aSt`) like a street's. Cost: in a city about 15-20% more stones than the streets alone (Hartwick, seed 3: 52k against 45k stones in the same tiles), baked in the same time slices. `OWR.old` / `OWQ.on=false` give the streets of upgrade 01 only, for comparison. The square of a big town (tier 4 and 5, `owqBig`) is small cobbles all over, without the band and the rings round its well, and the ground tiles paint it the same; where any square meets grass (nothing paved, no earth path, no other square and no building beyond its edge) its stones turn slightly olive, and the joints of every square are mossy towards its edge (upgrade 06b).

## Paths and grass
After a style reference (soft sandy paths in lush stylized grass): the unpaved ground of the roads, streets, lanes and paths (and the earth of the small squares) is clean light sand (`OWG_COL`; the earth part of `OW_GGLSL`): a touch lighter along the middle where it is walked, broad soft patches, darker and a little greener towards the edges where the grass leans in, and none of the old wheel ruts, pebbles, twigs or litter. The width of an unpaved path wanders by about a quarter either way (`owgWob`, `owgWVis`: a smooth noise of the place, visual only: the generator, the grid and the roads keep their numbers; paved streets keep their exact edges) and its edge into the grass is soft and wavy (the coverage of the ground tile broken by a noise per pixel). The terrain's own green is richer (`owTerrCol`) and only a trace of the old brown halo is left along the roads.
The grass (`src/grass.js`, environment upgrades 02 and 03; far off, with a Detail distance past Standard, a far carpet - see Detail distance) replaces the old sparse tufts of the foliage (`FOL_MAX.grass` is 0; flowers, ferns, shrubs, saplings, mushrooms and reeds stay): three kinds of plant after the style reference - a carpet of clumps of ten broad curved blades, patches of clover (groups of three round leaflets) and here and there a broad-leaved plant with big arching folded leaves and two tall blades standing out - growing right up to the wandering edge of every path (the same `owgWVis`), a little taller there, swaying in waves of wind. Shading (`grsMat`, one program, a material a kind for its height): darker towards the ground and inside each plant (an occlusion by height), bright at the top, the sunlight shining through the leaves when the camera looks towards the sun (computed with the vertex lighting, kept in the sun's shadow), both sides of a leaf lit as its front (the normals point up, so the grass is lit as one soft mass), the shadows of trees and houses received; the terrain between the plants is a deep green (`owTerrCol`), so the plants stand out against it as in the reference. Never on a path, a square, a building, a field, a prop, a stream bed, steep or high rocky ground or the keep's approach; positions from a seeded stream per cell, never the world's (world-sig 24/24). Cost: 16 m cells round the camera (`GRS.cs`), one instanced mesh per kind of plant in each, baked 2.5 ms a frame nearest first (`grsBake` is a generator), culled per cell; the plants drawn in a cell ease off with its distance (`GRS.D` the densities), beyond 8 m (`GRS.NEAR`) a cell draws the plain plants (single-triangle blades and leaflets, flat leaves) and beyond 20 m (`GRS.BROAD`) no broad-leaved plants; the Foliage Details setting scales density and distance (`GRS.LV`: low 20 m to ultra 46 m). On high: about 3,900 plants and 90,000 triangles in a countryside view, 25 to 30 draw calls, frame time within about a millisecond (`tests/grass-perf.js`).
Blending and merging (environment upgrade 04): roads that run side by side - many village-to-village roads share a corridor and were drawn as two or three bands a metre or two apart, overlapping into a ragged braid - are drawn as one: when the ground network is laid (`owgFinish`), each road is pulled onto a street or an earlier road that runs within 5.5 m of it and nearly along it (`owgMerge` in `src/roadstones.js`; all or nothing, short gaps closed and short touches dropped, eased over about ten metres at each end so it forks off in a gentle curve). Visual only: the roads of the world (`OW.roads`) keep their points (generation, props, the grid and the minimap use them), only the drawn copy that the ground tiles, the stones and the grass use is moved; on seed 3 the drawn braid goes from 411 m to 0 (`tests/path-braid.js`). The sand edge goes over into the grass through darker damp sand and patches of trampled grass and earth, with a softer wavy alpha, and a few grass clumps creep up to a third of a metre onto paths and streets (not the broad-leaved plants). The stones at the edge of a paved road where it meets open ground (no other paved road, square or wall beyond) are mossy and dirty in patches (a per-stone tint, `wearAt` in `owrBake`), and the joints near the edge are mossy (`OW_GGLSL`). The ground tiles also carry `aE` per vertex - the distance to the visual edge of the nearest path or square, and the least distance from a path's middle over its half width - fields that stay continuous where paths meet: the soft edge, the sand shading and the polished middle of a street are worked out per pixel from them (from the coverage and |lat|/w of the strongest path, interpolated over the 0.8 m cells, junctions and the mouths of squares were drawn with bright bands and a sawtooth along the triangles).

## Tier-3 buildings in depth
Every building of a tier-3 village is modelled in depth, inside and out (environment upgrade 08, `src/hut3d.js`; `owMakeBuilding` records the village's tier on the plan, `pl.vt`, and every hook is gated by `h3T3(o)`: `o.vt===3`, not a tier-1 hut, `H3.on`, `H3T.on`). ## The shadows of the people in the firelight
Inside a house the hero and the villagers of the room cast real shadows from the fire or lamp that lights it (the source of `interior/light.js`): each falls away from the flame, onto the floor, the walls, the furniture, the beds and the stairs, turns as its figure turns and swings with every stride, sharp at the feet and soft further off (the flame has a size, `OWHS_SIZE`, and the penumbra grows with the distance behind the figure). A fire pit in the middle of a room throws everyone's shadow outwards; a villager asleep by the hearth casts too; the hero's body also casts in first person, where it is not drawn. How: the point light's cube map holds only what does not move and is drawn only when the source changes, so the figures are drawn every frame into a cube map of their own seen from the flame (`OWHS`: a point light that is never added to the scene, so no shader of the world gets another light; only its shadow map is drawn, after the other shadow maps, `owhsPass`). It sees nothing but the hero and the villagers on the hero's floor of the house of the source (or in its doorway), nearest the flame first, eight at most (`owhsCasters`); only the faces of the cube that see one of them are drawn (`owhsFaces`), the hero's skinned meshes are culled face by face with the sphere of the whole hero for this pass, and the hero's parts under 6 cm are left out. The shaders of the house (`lightmap.js`, the materials with `USE_LM`) read it only inside a cone round each figure (`uHSC`): the real light of the source is cut where someone stands between it and the surface, and so is the warm light of the light grid (most of a fire's light in a room), but only where the source itself reaches (its own cube shadow, `owFv`), so a shadow never shows through a partition, and only in the house of the source (`uK2.w`). The programs that draw skinned figures into a cube map are compiled by one empty pass as a world comes up. The source's point light is drawn in its own pass with the walls and the furniture only (`userData.owLayer3`, which it had lost): before, it was drawn in the ordinary pass and froze the hero and any villager into its map where they stood when the source was picked. Cost: about 1 ms a frame for the hero alone and 2 ms with two villagers (CPU and GPU, measured in a hidden test pane), only while the hero is within the reach of a source.

Every part of an interior is a closed solid, so a face seen from behind can only be seen through that cut; the interior shader (`owlmPatch` in `interior/lightmap.js`) now draws such a face as the top the cut leaves: the colour of its own part, a level normal, the light grid read straight above the point and only the light of the room (the grid's, the sky's, the fires' warm light; no lamps, no sun, no shadows, which inside a solid would mark the walls of the cut). No new shader programs.

## Lamps and lights
The five lamp posts (`lampKinds` in `owTownDress`): Tier 1 a wooden torch post with an iron cresset, Tier 2 a post on a stone foot with an iron arm, brace and a hanging lantern, Tier 3 a dark post on a plinth with a longer arm and a hooded lantern, Tier 4 a cast-iron post with collars, a cross-bar and a lantern on a seat, Tier 5 a brass-collared post with three lanterns (one at each end of the cross-bar and one on top). A lantern is a cage (corner posts, base plate, hood) round a lit core in the glow layer; each kind returns where its flames are, so the halo, the pool of light and the real light sit at the flame, not at the foot of the post. The cage is a mesh of its own (`q`): it casts the sun's shadows but not the shadows of the light inside it (a light in a cage that cast would stand in the shadow of its own roof).
`OW.lamps` has one entry per LANTERN ({x, y, z at the flame, s (1 street, .5 on a building), post (which post it hangs on), li (its share of the light), hs (halo size), pool (does it have a ground pool)}); a post with three lanterns has three. `OWLT` (see `owLampUpdate`, `owLampPick`): a pool of 6 real point lights follows the lanterns nearest to the hero (and what the camera faces), re-chosen every 5 frames with a margin (a bound lantern keeps its light until it is clearly out of the best group, so two lanterns of the same distance never take turns) and with fades (a light fades out before it moves and fades in on the next lantern; the weight of a slot follows what it stands for over a quarter of a second; `tests/lamp-stable.js` walks 140 m along a street and reads the lights every frame). The nearest 3 lanterns each have a light with a SHADOW MAP of its own (512 cube map, range 24 m, only layer 3: the building proxies, walls, props, the lamp posts; drawn once when the light is bound and again, one light per frame, when something that casts changes: a house damaged, repaired or entered, a door moving, `owLampShadowDirty`), so the three lanterns of a post throw three overlapping shadows of the same post and the fence posts and walls round it throw each their own. The next posts have plain lights (3, range 16, no shadow) that stand for a whole post (the lanterns of a post that have no shadow light are added up in one light at their middle): the cheaper picture of the same light. The shadow lights are in the scene only at night (a shadow light costs a lookup for every pixel it reaches); the first frames of a world draw it with and without them and with and without the light of the interiors, so that every shader variant is compiled before the first dusk (58 programs at noon, at night and inside a house).
Every lamp also has a soft pool of light on the ground (one cheap mesh per village, additive) for the lamps that are too far to have a real light; a point on its rim that a house hides from the lamp gets almost none (the lamp does not light the ground behind a house through the house: `owLampPools`), and it fades out where a real light stands (the real light and its shadows are not filled in by it); the glowing fixture and its halo are seen out to the render distance. Hamlets get a few modest lamps, cities lanterns on every street, market and building. Day/night comes from `OWLT.n`.
Fall-off (`interior/lightmap.js`, the `bsdfs` chunk): every point light with decay 2 uses an inverse square law with a soft core and a smooth window to zero at its range, 7.1 / (1 + (d / (0.085 range))^2) times the window, instead of three.js' (1 - d / range)^2 (nearly flat over the first third of the range: a lantern lit a whole room evenly). A quarter of the light is left at 0.17 of the range, a tenth at 0.27 and none at the range, and the point shadows are read with four taps instead of nine. What the lights add to the ground round a three-lantern post (`tests/lamp-falloff.js`): 94, 61, 26, 12, 2 and 0 at 1.5, 3, 5, 8, 12 and 17 m, on a moonlit ground of 40 to 54.
Why the back of a house used to be lit by the lanterns of its front (`tests/rear-leak.js`: the ground 3.5 m behind a house and the wall of its back, with the parts of the lamp light switched off one by one): five real lights of 30 m range with no shadow at all lit the ground behind every house through the house (+19 on a ground of 36, a half more), the pools of light on the ground ignored the houses (up to 12.5 m), and the fall-off was flat. Now the ground behind is +4 on 36 (what the lamps behind it give), the wall of the back +1 on 7.

## Performance
`src/batch.js` merges the intact buildings of a settlement, by 30 m cell, into one mesh per cell and their glass into another; a building that is damaged, ruined or being explored switches back to its own meshes through a per-vertex flag (`aS`). Buildings cast shadows through a plain proxy (`owAddShadowProxy`), the shadow cascade samples only the map that matters at a pixel, and the near cascade is refreshed on every other frame while the frame rate is below ~43 fps. `tests/perf-world.js` (`__pw.pose`, `__pw.gpu`, `__pw.measure`) and `tests/perf-compare.js` take reproducible measurements (GPU timer queries on `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`).

## Props: shapes
The round things of `props.js` are turned or built with many sides (the ground view is flat shaded, so a ring of 20 reads as round where a ring of 9 reads as a gem): barrels (36 staves, 20-sided hoops and heads), buckets (14 staves, a rope handle that is an arc of short pieces, not a dotted ring), baskets, sacks (one turned body with a gathered, puckered mouth), pottery (three turned profiles, `lathe`: a jar, a pot, a jug with a handle) and the shrubs of the planters. The shapes come from a hash of the place, not from the world's stream; the draws of the old props are unchanged. `tests/prop-sheet.js` renders a sheet, `prop-ground.js` checks contact with the ground, `prop-valid.js` counts props that overlap each other, stand inside a house or on the way up to a door (1,000 props of 4 seeds: only the axe in a chopping block, which is meant).

## The inventory (I)
Asked 2026-10-13: "pressing i will open an inventory that will be 6x3, 6 columns and 3 lines; pressing I again or Esc will exit from it". `#inv`: a panel in the middle of the screen over the dimmed world (above the top bar and the hotbar, under the menus), 18 slots in 6 columns and 3 rows (`#invGrid`, the hotbar's slot style; 44 px under 440 px wide, 38 px under 340 px). ## The first item: the sword
Asked 2026-10-13: "lets add first item, a sword, basic default 3d sword that you can equip by having it in the selected hotbar and your hero will hold it". Decided with the user: the bow is removed for now (the hero only uses what the hotbar holds) and the sword hangs in its scabbard at the hip when its slot is not selected. The items are listed in `ITEMS` (name, icon, weapon); a slot stores `{id}` and `itemDef(item)` looks the rest up, so saves carry no pictures. Every adventure starts with the sword in hotbar slot 1, selected; the hotbar's slots and selection now belong to the adventure (`hot` in serializeRun, `hotLoad` in loadRun and resetRun; a run saved before gets the starting sword; the old per-player `meta.hot` is no longer read). `pcHold()` is the weapon of the selected slot's item: before the character's rig moves (animHero) `pcHoldSync` draws the sword while it is selected (it keeps it drawn: the rig would put it away 8 s after the last fight) and puts it away otherwise; after, a hero with no sword in the hotbar or the inventory wears no scabbard. The model is the character's own sword (pcm.js kit: blade, gold guard, leather grip, pommel; in the right hand, or in the scabbard at the left hip). With empty hands the hero seeks, holds and strikes no foe (heroUpdate). `pcWeapon()` (pc.js) is always 'sword' (class blade); X (switch) and R (draw) are gone and the keys can be bound to scrolls; the hints no longer name them.

## The sword's animations (src/pcsword.js)
Asked 2026-10-13: "in idle he has his arm next to him as if nothing is equipped and he's holding the sword (the default idle); after pressing left click he swings the sword and enters combat mode, holding the sword up in a defensive position; after 5 seconds of no combat he puts it back down; 10 swinging animations, 5 combat defensive idles and 3 non-combat idles". `pcSwordPose` runs inside the character's controller (pcanim.js, after the arms' stance; pcanim.js only calls it, skips its own attack overlay for these swings, shows the sword in the hand while `t.pcHeld`, takes the sink into the pelvis before the legs, and lets a fight end after 5 s instead of 8). At rest the arm is the empty hand's (it hangs and swings with the walk) and the hand holds the blade forward and down; standing still, now and then the blade rests on the shoulder or is lifted to look along the edge (`PCSI`). A left click (mouse, the sword in the hand, the open world) plays one of the ten swings (`PCSW`: overhead chop, forehand and backhand slashes, two descending diagonals, a rising cut, a thrust, a whirl over the head into a slash, a lunge, a double cut), never the same twice running, towards where the camera looks or the foe in reach; a foe within reach in front is struck at the swing's moment (`heroFire`), and the hero's own attacks use the same swings. A swing or a foe held starts a fight: the sword comes up into one of five guards (`PCSG`: middle, high, hanging, low, ox; another now and then), breathing in it, the off hand up; 5 s after the last of the fight it goes back down. Each pose gives the grip (in the chest's frame from the sword shoulder, in arm lengths), the blade's direction and its edge, the body's turn, lean and sink, the off hand and the head; the arm reaches the grip by two-bone IK, the hand turns the blade with the forearm taking most of the twist; a swing runs on a Catmull-Rom arc through its keys from wherever the sword was into the guard. The hints say "click to swing". The scabbard (pcm.js) is now as long as the blade: the sheathed blade's tip used to stand out below it.
