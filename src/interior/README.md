# Kingsgaard interiors

Procedural, walkable building interiors. Built from the existing building architecture (the `arch` record that `owArch` writes for every house), not beside it.
Spliced into the game by `build.py` (see the `/*@include interior/...*/` lines in `src/kingsgaard.html`). Nine files, loaded in this order:

| file | what it does |
|---|---|
| `core.js` | `OWI` (settings, the `OWI.register` interface), local/world transforms, the **entrance** (steps from the terrain up to the floor, `owiEntrance` / `owiPlanEnter`), zones, the occupancy-grid carve, the audit that closes doors that cannot be reached, box colliders (`owiHit`, `owiPush`) |
| `plan.js` | `owiRead` (what the building already is: storeys, door, windows, stairs, in metres), `owiLayout` (floor plan first: rooms, partitions, doorways, stair bay) |
| `place.js` | reserved zones (way in, door leaf, stairs, doorways), the placement engine (`owiPlace`, `owiSeat`, `owiDeco`): every piece is tried against walls, windows, reservations and other pieces, and kept only if every doorway/room/stair stays reachable |
| `furnish.js` | what goes in which room (living, hearth, beds, tavern bar and kitchen, scroll shop counter / racks / workshop), the **walkability proof** (`owiProve`: flood fill with the hero's real radius), `owiMakePlan` |
| `furnish2.js` | the rooms of trades and halls: goods shop (`owiGoods`), forge (`owiForge`), carpenter (`owiCarp`), storeroom (`owiStoreroom`), great hall (`owiGreatHall`), council chamber (`owiCouncil`) |
| `furniture.js` | the meshes of about 35 pieces (beds, tables, benches, hearths, kegs, scroll racks, an orb...) in two merged meshes: lit and glowing |
| `furniture2.js` | the pieces of the trades (anvil, forge, lathe, shop counters, goods, storeroom stacks, banners, sconces...) and the **chests**: three kinds (worn board chest, ironbound chest, rich chest with brass), every board its own part with its own tone, a lid with a seam, thickness and a half-round top, hinges, hasp, lock, corner straps, rivets and scratches (the last three only on pieces near the player). The chest parts are also what the classic board's treasure chest is built from (`chestModel`) |
| `geo.js` | the shell of an interior: floors, partitions with doorways, stairs, rails, the passage walls; the entrance steps; load / unload of the meshes |
| `walk.js` | colliders from the plan, the zones, hero movement (`owiStep`), height under the feet (`owiHeroY`), the cutaway, loading and unloading around the hero (`owiTick`) |

## How it fits together
* **One frame.** Everything is written in the building's local frame in metres (x right, z towards the door, y up from the foundation base). A rotated building, a building on a slope, or a house in a row needs no special case: `owiL2W` / `owiW2L` are the only conversions.
* **Seeded.** The plan of a building depends on the world seed, where the building stands and what it is (`owiSeed`), so it is the same every time, and different from its neighbours. Unloading the meshes keeps the plan.
* **The way in is one chain.** terrain -> steps -> foundation ledge -> sill -> floor. The floor is raised until the first two metres of approach lie above the ground (`owiNeedHi`), the number of steps comes from the real terrain height at the foot (`owiEntrance`, risers at most 19 cm, treads 40 cm), and the same numbers make the step meshes, the cheek walls, the colliders and the height under the walker. The occupancy grid is carved (`owiCarve`) so that the footprint margin that the village layout paints does not wall the steps in.
* **Collision follows the plan.** Walls, the foundation ledge, cheek walls, porch posts, furniture and stair sides are axis-aligned boxes with a level mask. The hero is a circle that is pushed out of any box it overlaps (`owiPush`), in sub-steps of 10 cm, so walls are slid along and corners rounded. Outside the zones of a building nothing changes: the old grid rule applies.
* **Stairs.** The straight flights that `owPlanStairs` generated are used, in metres, and `owiRead` adapts them to the house: the flights keep to the wall the front door is not on; a house too shallow for the drawn flight (riser 18 cm, tread 27 cm) gets a steeper one (riser up to 21 cm, tread down to 22 cm); a flight starts at least `OWI.STAIRFRONT` (90 cm) from the front wall (the architecture leaves 1 m; a shallow house gives some of it up) so a walker can come up to the first step; a house too narrow for the 1.05 m strip beside rooms of `OWI.MINBESIDE` (1 m) gets the same flights `OWI.NARROW` (92 cm) wide; and a house that still cannot hold every flight gets as many upper floors as fit (`P.fl`). A flight is kept only if there is at least 72 cm of floor beyond its top end. Walking on a flight follows its slope, and the level changes half way. The steps have a mesh of their own that no cutaway touches.
* **Cutaway** (third person only; first person and the free camera cut nothing, `OWI.cutOn`). Three clips, all gliding: the interior (floors, furniture) is clipped 1.5 m above the floor under the hero (`b.cutPlane`); the outside of the house (walls, roof, upper storeys, glass) above the ceiling of the hero's storey (`b.topPlane`), so the walls of that storey stay and only the roof and the storeys above go; and the outer walls and the partitions on the camera's side of the hero by a vertical plane through the hero that turns with the camera (`b.camPlane`, `owiCamPlane`). The floor, the far walls and every neighbouring building are untouched. Entering and leaving have hysteresis, and the planes go back off when the hero leaves. Where the clip of the interior cuts a piece of furniture or a partition (a chimney breast, a cupboard, a tall shelf), the cut is closed: every part of an interior is a closed solid, so a face seen from behind can only be seen through the cut, and the interior shader (`owlmPatch`) draws it as the top the cut leaves - the colour of its part, a level normal, the light grid read straight above, only the light of the room (no lamps, no sun, no shadows).
* **Doors** (`door.js`). The front door of every enterable house is shut at the start. F (PC) opens and closes the one door the hero stands at (a prompt says which; the camera's heading decides between two doors; a wall of this house or a neighbour's is not looked through); on a touch screen the door opens when the hero comes up to it and shuts when they walk away. The leaf hangs on one jamb (`owLeafHinge` picks the jamb, away from the stairs; `owiHinge` in plan.js takes the other one when the ground floor could not be walked round the leaf standing open, and the plan's proof walks round it: `P.leafBox`), pinned 6 mm inside it at the corner of the leaf on the side it swings towards, so the hinge edge only ever moves away from the frame; opened from outside it swings into the house (the plan keeps that corner free), from inside out over the steps, away from the hero, and falls back to inwards when something stands in the outward sweep. One box fills the doorway while the door is shut or moving; a thin box takes the open leaf's place (slots `col.dA` / `col.dB`, `owdSyncCol`). Closing is refused with the hero in the doorway. A moving or open door is drawn by its own pivot and its building uses its own meshes (the batches hold only shut doors). The state is the fourth element of each building's save entry (0 shut, 1 open inwards, 2 outwards); a door nobody is near (80 m from the hero and the camera) shuts by itself.
* **Loading.** Plans are made 1 per 0.1 s for the nearest building within `OWI.LOAD` (62 m), meshes are built at the same pace and released beyond `OWI.UNLOAD` (100 m); in a Tier 5 city about 12 of 580 interiors exist at once. Plan about 3 ms on average (up to 20 ms for a three-storey house), mesh about 1 ms. A building's glass is see-through only while its interior is loaded (`owGlassMode`); beyond that it is a dark pane.
* **Furnished and enterable are different things.** `OWI.reg` holds every furnished building (what is seen through the windows), `OWI.list` only those the hero can walk into. A furnished building that is not enterable (the great hall: `OWI.noEnter`) is planned and meshed like the others but has no entrance, steps or colliders.
* **Night.** A stable share of houses (by the plan's seed and the tier, `ic.lit`, `owiLitOf`) is lived in: its far windows glow after dark (`OW_GLASS_FARLIT`, scaled by the day/night factor `OWLT.n`) and, when the hero is inside, the rooms have a virtual lamp in the light grid, so a street is a mix of lit and dark windows rather than uniformly bright.
* **Light between the floors and behind furniture** (`owlmSources`, `owlmThrough`, `boxHit` in `lightmap.js`): a source on one floor puts light into the opening of a flight of stairs, which comes out of the opening on the floor above or below as a weaker, softer source (`OWLM.thru`); a tall piece of furniture stops the baked light of a low fire for the floor behind it. The solid floors, the walls and the front door's leaf still stop light (they are on layer 3, the layer of the shadow lights; the shadow lights of the lanterns outside are drawn again when the door moves). Interior doorways have no door leaves.
* **The light grid** (`lightmap.js`): a baked grid per floor (16 cm cells, two textures) of the daylight from each face of the house, the daylight through the front door, the lamps and the fires, and ambient occlusion; the interior meshes and the walls sample it (`owlmPatch`, `USE_LM`). The main README has the whole pipeline. `owlmBake` runs at most once per frame within 3 ms (`owlmTick`), `owlmPrime` compiles the four shader variants before the first entry, `OWLM.prof` marks the bake in the performance timeline.
* **Passages.** A row house with a carriage passage (Tier 5) is a tunnel: steps at both ends, solid masses either side, a stone-walled corridor, a plank ceiling.

* **Floor plans differ within an archetype.** The plan is chosen from the building's seed through a stream of its own (`P.RL`, `owiLayout`), so it is the same every time and never disturbs the furnishing's random numbers. A house, a stone house or a row house is cut front to back with the kitchen at the back (as before), front to back with the kitchen at the front, or side to side with the kitchen on the outer side; a cottage has a bedroom at the back, a bedroom beside the hall, or one room; the depth and width of the rooms vary; upstairs the number of bedrooms and their depths vary and a Tier 3+ house may have a study (`study`: desk, shelves, chest). In a sample world the number of distinct room arrangements for row houses rose from 16 to 71 (Tier 4) and from 13 to 86 (Tier 5); `tests/int-variety.js`.
* **Beds in depth** (`furniture4.js`, after `furniture3.js`): soft closed shells (mattress, pillows, a made blanket that falls over the sides, the sheet turned down, a throw), turned posts, panels, gathered curtains; their made bedding is part `b` / `bL` / `bR`, remembered by the furniture mesh (`ic.bedR`) and hidden under a sleeper's own covers (vilhome.js). See the main README.
* **Furniture has variants** (`furniture3.js`). Tables (trestle, planked with turned legs, apron, carved with a cloth runner), chairs (plain, ladder back, spindle back, armchair), stools (round, square, milking, cushioned), benches (slab, four-legged, settle), beds (box, rope, post, curtained), chests (flat, plank with iron corners, domed, trunk), cupboards (two doors, panelled, dresser, tall press), shelves, barrels, crates and rugs each come in two to four constructions, chosen from the piece's seed and weighted by the wealth of the house (Tier 1-2 worn and simple, 4-5 turned, carved and panelled). New wall pieces: a rail of pots, a shelf of books, a wreath. All rest on the floor (`tests/ground-items.js`).

* **One flight, one mesh.** Every flight of a multi-storey house is a mesh of its own (`ic.flights`, layer `s<id>` in `owiBuildStairs`) and `owiStairVis` shows the flights of the hero's floor (`owiFlightsAt`): the flight that leaves it upwards and the one that arrives from below. `tests/stair-floors.js` walks every flight of houses with 2 to 4 floors and checks the set drawn in every frame.
* **Fallback furnishing** (`fallback.js`, after the primary rules and their proof): a room with no piece gets 1 to 5 fitting ones; a closet one metre wide has its doorway strip relaxed to half a metre and takes only pieces thin enough to leave 60 cm free; undone newest first when the proof of walkability fails.
* **Lights and shadows** (`light.js`, geo.js, walk.js). The furniture is layer `c`: a mesh that casts, enabled only for the house the hero is in (`owiCastOn`). `owiRegister` sets `clipShadows` off on the outside of an enterable house and `owiMaterial(..., full)` does the same for furniture and partitions: the cutaway is a view, not a change of the light.
* **The shadows of the people in the firelight** (`light.js` `OWHS`, `lightmap.js`): the hero and the villagers on the hero's floor of the house of the room's source are drawn every frame into a cube map of their own seen from the source (a point light kept out of the scene: only its shadow map is used; only the faces that see someone are drawn), and the `USE_LM` shaders cut both the source's real light and the warm light of the grid where someone stands in the way, looked up only in a cone round each figure (`uHSC`), the grid's light only where the source's own cube shadow says it reaches (`owFv`) and only in the house of the source (`uK2.w`). The source's own cube map sees layer `OWIL_LAYER` only (`userData.owLayer3`), so nothing that moves is frozen into it. See the main README.

* **Ceilings** (geo.js `owiBuildCeiling`, `owiRoofShape` in core.js): see the main README. `ic.rs` is the shape of the roof, `ic.dip` the lowest reach of the roof under the top of the walls, `owiCeilH` / `owiCeilAt` the height of a ceiling over the floor; `it.ceil` is taken at the piece's own place.
* **Solid buildings and pavilions** (`pl.solid`, `pl.open`): enterable only in the sense of the pipeline: `owiSolidPlan` gives a plan with one empty floor, `owiBuildCol` a front wall without a doorway (solid) or `owpBuildCol` (pavilion.js) the edge of the platform, the cheek walls, posts and counters.

## Which buildings are furnished and enterable
`OWI.types` (furnished): hut, cottage, stone cottage, town house, house, timber house, stone house, tavern, scroll merchant, goods merchant, smith, carpenter, storage house, great hall, the row houses (`rowh`, `rowc`, with party walls respected) and the passage houses of Tier 5. All of them are enterable except the hall (`OWI.noEnter`). Barns, stables, sheds, wells and shrines have no interior. A building without a usable front door (`noDoor`, `noFill0`) is not furnished.

## Adding a building type
```js
OWI.register('smith','house');                       // a smithy uses the house interior
OWI.register('smith','forge',{                       // or its own layout and furniture
  layout(P,F,t){ /* t = {W_,room,away,hallRects,M,C,mw,md,Q,PT,R,owiRect}: cut F into rooms */ },
  furnish(S,room){ /* place pieces with owiPlace / owiSeat / owiDeco; return true when done */ return false; }
});
```
Register before the world is built (`OWI.canEnter` is asked while buildings are made). A new piece of furniture is one entry in `OWI_ITEM` (footprint) and one in `OWI_MESH` (drawing).
The type's `OWB` plan must have a front door and a clear front (the same conditions the other enterable types meet).

## Known limits
* Furniture is not interactive (no containers, chests do not open); only the front doors open, and their state is saved.
* Interiors are lit by the sun (real shadows through the windows), the sky light scaled by a baked grid (windows, door, partitions, ambient occlusion), the fires and lamps of the rooms (baked, plus one real shadowed point light for the nearest source) and nothing else: no bounce light, no coloured bleeding, and the grid is baked from the plan (furniture added later does not darken it until the building is loaded again). The interior doorways have no door leaves, so light moves between rooms through them; only the front door opens and shuts.
* The upper floors of a passage house are not enterable yet.
* A floor whose two stair flights enclose a corner narrower than the hero (a three-storey town house) keeps that corner empty; `owiProve` counts only pockets that furniture adds (`F.pk`).
* Interiors beyond 62 m do not exist (nothing can be seen through a window of a far house): a 1500 m world with ~5000 furnished buildings cannot hold them all.
* A village layout that leaves a neighbour's wall across a door's approach closes that door (`owiAudit`); about 1 in 150 houses.
* In a dense city the third-person camera can still end up inside a building on the opposite side of a narrow street (not changed by this system).
* The narrowest Tier 5 row houses (3 m inside) cannot hold two stair lanes beside a room: of the 466 multi-storey houses of a sample world 324 have every flight, the rest (the narrow `rowc` and some `rowh` rows) have as many reachable floors as fit.
* The party wall of a row house belongs to the taller neighbour and is not cut away with the house the hero is in.
